# Features and coverage (“1 Molecule” – “4 Input preview”, Result viewer, History, MCP)

MolForma is a desktop application for building molecules, editing coordinates, preparing calculation input, running calculations and viewing results for NWChem. Molecular coordinates are kept as double-precision values in Å, and atoms are referenced by persistent IDs. It runs and stops calculations locally, shows their logs and results, lists the history of saved calculations, shows their input and logs, and reuses their molecules and settings. It can also be driven from MCP clients ([MCP server](#mcp-server)).

## Getting started: the basic workflow

1. **Build a molecule (“1 Molecule”)**: Use “From SMILES” or “Open” (for a structure file) in the file-name menu at the left of the header (or in the “File” menu), or use the parts palette. Common elements have buttons in the palette (H, C, N, O, F, P, S, Cl, Br, I); choose any other element with “Periodic table…”. Clicking an atom or part in the palette switches to the “Place” tool, and you place it by clicking in the 3D view. When you choose a tool in the column at the left edge (or with keys such as S, A, B), its settings appear above the 3D view and what a click does appears below it. Changes such as deletion, cleanup and ligands are shown as candidates: “Confirm changes” (Ctrl+Enter) applies them, Esc discards them and Ctrl+Z undoes them.
2. **Set up the calculation (“2 Calculation”)**: Choose the task, method, basis set, charge and multiplicity. Problems are listed on the right; those marked “→ Double-click to fix” can be fixed by double-clicking them. To see orbital shapes, turn on “Write MOLDEN file” in “Orbitals and output files”. Opening “Advanced settings” in a section lets you set items that go directly into the NWChem blocks.
3. **Run the calculation**: “Run calculation” at the right of the header or “Calculation → Run calculation” (F5) starts NWChem (check the executable and other settings in “3 Run settings”). When the run ends, the result is loaded into the “Result viewer”, and the optimization convergence, energy and number of imaginary frequencies are shown in the run panel (“Run status and log”) and in the status bar.
4. **View the results (“Result viewer”, “History”)**: Check the structure, energy, orbitals, vibrations and more in the tabs that have data. Open earlier calculations from “History” to reuse their molecules and settings.

The following sections describe the features and their limitations. Keyboard operations are listed in “Help → Keyboard shortcuts”.

## 3D rendering

- The molecule editor and the result viewer use the same rendering component. Builds that detect a Qt6-compatible VTK embed a C++ VTK scene in `QVTKOpenGLNativeWidget`. There is no runtime dependence on Python or PyVista.
- Only when OpenGL 3.2 is unavailable and rendering falls back from VTK to QPainter does each 3D view show “Rendering: QPainter (CPU) · simplified, OpenGL unavailable” at its top left (this also follows a later switch caused by a failed initialization). The current renderer is shown in “Help → About MolForma”, and you can also choose QPainter under “3D rendering (after restart)” in “Preferences”. VTK rendering does not guarantee that a hardware GPU is used.
- Atoms are drawn together as GPU glyphs. Isosurfaces are kept as separate meshes for the positive and negative parts, and their vertices and normals are updated only when the shape changes. Normals are computed per vertex from the gradient of the grid values, so the shading hides the edges between triangles. Rotation, zoom, pan, opacity, toggling the positive/negative parts and selection do not rebuild isosurfaces. The meshes are also kept when a redisplayed result frame uses the same shared mesh. Orbital evaluation and isosurface generation still run in a separate, cancellable process.
- Orthographic projection and the coordinate transforms used for editing are shared, and camera operations do not change physical coordinates. Double and triple bonds, aromatic dashes and coordination-bond arrows update only small drawing-only coordinates so that they stay visible from the current direction. The displayed atom radii are limited to the same on-screen range as before.
- Labels, selection outlines, fixed marks, ghosts, the small handles of hidden hydrogens, coordination sites, terminal handles, point charges and coordinate axes are drawn in a Qt 2D layer. This layer always appears in front of the 3D shapes. Mouse, keyboard and drag-and-drop input is handled by the existing editing code.
- Translucent surfaces request up to 16 depth-peeling passes; MSAA is disabled and FXAA is used. Where many surfaces overlap, or with drivers that do not support it, transparency compositing has limits. Even with OpenGL, software implementations such as llvmpipe are not as fast as a hardware GPU. Isosurfaces are limited to 500,000 triangles. The QPainter path evenly decimates surfaces with more than 150,000 triangles.
- QPainter rendering is used with `MOLFORMA_RENDERER=painter`, in builds without VTK, on Qt's offscreen/minimal platforms and when the OpenGL context fails to initialize. The QPainter path also caches the camera matrix, atom colors and bond endpoint lookups, but large isosurfaces are limited by CPU rendering.
- “View → Display style” switches between Ball and stick (default), Stick, Space-filling (CPK, Open Babel van der Waals radii) and Wireframe. In Stick and Wireframe, each bond is colored by the atoms at its two ends, and multiple bonds are drawn as a single line. The chosen style is saved in the app settings and does not affect the molecule data or calculations.
- “View → Hide selected atoms” (Ctrl+Shift+H) hides atoms temporarily, and “Show all atoms” (Ctrl+Shift+U) shows them again. This is a display-only setting: hidden atoms are still saved, used in calculations and included when a selection is expanded to its unit. Hidden atoms cannot be selected by clicking.
- “View → Show measurement of selection in view” (Ctrl+M) keeps the distance, angle or dihedral of 2–4 atoms selected in order on the view, as a purple dashed line with its value. The value follows atom movements and disappears when one of the atoms is deleted. “Clear measurements in view” removes them. Measurements are not saved in the project.
- “View → Show hydrogen bonds” draws blue dashed lines between an H bonded to N, O or F and another N, O or F (H···Y 1.5–2.6 Å, X–H···Y at least 120°; up to 5000 atoms). This is a guide based on distance and angle, not an energetic criterion.

Regular CTest runs need no display server and test the QPainter path together with editing, candidates and undo. The VTK path can be tested separately for display positions and click coordinates, translucency, isosurface retention during rotation and PNG capture.

```sh
xvfb-run -a env QT_QPA_PLATFORM=xcb MOLFORMA_REQUIRE_VTK=1 \
  MOLFORMA_RENDER_ARTIFACTS=/tmp/molforma-rendering ./build/test_rendering
```

Compare rendering performance with the same display environment, view size and shapes. The following optional benchmark rotates 10,000, 50,000 and 100,000 triangles in an 800×600 view and prints the median and the 95th percentile of 30 frames after a warm-up. The times are not used to pass or fail the test. Under Xvfb the values usually reflect software OpenGL; to assess a real GPU, run it on the actual display.

```sh
MOLFORMA_RENDER_BENCHMARK=1 ./build/test_rendering rotationBenchmark
MOLFORMA_RENDERER=painter MOLFORMA_RENDER_BENCHMARK=1 ./build/test_rendering rotationBenchmark
```

The roughly 16.7 ms of the VTK path corresponds to the 60 Hz sync of this display and does not represent the processing time of the GPU itself.

| --- | --- | --- |
| 10,000 | 22.29ms | 16.71ms |

## Right-side pages of “1 Molecule” and NWChem support

The right side has six pages, “Edit”, “Transform”, “Attributes” (atom attributes, ghosts and fixing only), “Coordination”, “Residue” and “Charge”, chosen with the switch at the top (there are no further tabs inside the pages). Each page groups its operations into cards, with buttons side by side. Frequently used operations have blue buttons (ligand placement, building from a sequence, adding point charges), and the less used “Site directions” opens with the arrow in its heading. What remains are the calculation settings passed directly to NWChem and the operations that build elements, coordinates, hydrogens and stereochemistry. Bond orders, residue names, coordination sites and oxidation states are not written directly to the molecular QM input of NWChem, but they are used to prepare the initial structure.

| Tab, item | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Edit: change element, XYZ of one atom and move to origin (in the “3D” layout), placement depth | Create the elements and coordinates passed to the calculation |
| Edit: bond type, bond 2 atoms, select one side of a bond, delete with hydrogen capping | Structure preparation that derives hydrogen counts and what moves from the bond graph |
| Transform: translate, rotate, invert stereocenter, switch E/Z, record R/S and E/Z | Create and check stereoisomers. A recorded R/S is shown in the atom's label in the 3D view (“C1 (S)”) and E/Z in the middle of the double bond (“(E)”); the status bar names what was recorded. The stereo labels themselves are not written to the NWChem input |
| Transform: align orientation, move the center of mass, principal axes, mirror and mirrored copy, duplicate | Create initial coordinates, isomers and complexes |
| Attributes: formal charge, unpaired electrons, apply attributes | Attributes for adding hydrogens and for the chemical structure. Shown as separate from the total charge and spin multiplicity of the calculation |
| Attributes: isotope or arbitrary mass, make ghost and remove ghost | Writes `mass`, `bq<element>` and the basis |
| Attributes: fix selected atoms, unfix | Protection during editing and `constraints / fix atom` in optimizations. Sets or clears fixing explicitly even when fixed and unfixed atoms are mixed. The context menu of the 3D view uses the same names |
| Distance, angle and dihedral constraints, scans | Set in “Coordinate constraints” and “IRC and coordinate scan” of “2 Calculation” (written to NWChem's `zcoord`). Distances and angles are changed and measured in “1 Molecule” |
| “Structure → Sort atoms” of the coordinate table (By element, By residue, Selected atoms first); saving, loading and “Remove from list” for “My parts” in the palette | Organize the output atom order and reuse structures |
| “Clean up all” above the 3D view, “Local cleanup” in the selection box of “Edit” (selected atoms only), “Tools → Regenerate 3D” | Initial structure preparation with Open Babel. Separate from the NWChem calculation |
| “File → Review settings held at loading…” | Read-only view of settings in the old format (for checking compatibility). Does not affect whether NWChem input can be generated |
| Coordination: coordination geometry, center–donor distance, site number, place, replace and move ligands, swap 2 sites, individual site directions | Create the initial structure of complexes. Check the candidate and confirm it |
| Coordination: auxiliary input of oxidation states and applying them | Notes that the total charge and multiplicity of the calculation are set separately in “2 Calculation” |
| Residue: recognition, completing missing atoms, building from a sequence, L/D, side-chain states, N/C-terminal states | Prepare the atoms, hydrogens, protonation states and conformation of peptides. Recognition from the bond graph matches standard amino acids by elements and connections (not by bond orders guessed from an XYZ file), also recognizes ACE and NME caps at the chain ends, and sets formal charges (COO−, NH3+, Lys, Arg, HIP histidine) and side-chain states (HID, HIE, HIP, charged or neutral Asp and Glu, …) from the hydrogens present. A total charge that matched the sum of formal charges before follows the new sum |
| Residue: adding at either end, mutating residues, applying terminal states, deleting, reconnecting, backbone and side-chain dihedrals | Edit the composition and the initial conformation. Mutation uses the state of the amino acid chosen in the palette (“Histidine (HIP, +1)” and so on), keeps the backbone N, Cα, C and O, and puts the backbone amide hydrogen into the peptide plane (opposite the previous C=O); adding an ACE cap does the same. The total charge follows the formal charges as when adding residues. Reconnecting needs a C–N distance of 1.1–1.7 Å; otherwise it gives the present distance and how to close it with a dimension |
| Residue: selecting from the list, selecting atoms without residue, manual registration | Select and manage large structures |
| Fragment assignment (FMO/Counterpoise/SAPT0), fragment charge and multiplicity, BDA:BAA | No UI to create or edit them (they cannot be converted to NWChem input). The counterpoise correction is set with “BSSE correction” in “2 Calculation” (monomers per bonded molecule, NWChem's `bsse` block). FMO and SAPT0 are not supported |
| Residue: PDB loading, diagnosis and repair, “Check structure…”, exporting the atom table as CSV | The CSV stores the output atom number, element, atom name and residue |
| Edit: 3D view coloring (element, residue; “Element” card) | Check the structure |
| Charge: display of the total charge and multiplicity, applying the sum of formal charges as the total charge | The values are set in “2 Calculation”; this page shows them and leads there with Change in “2 Calculation”. With coordination centers, “Use oxidation and formal charges (n)” counts their oxidation numbers (aid) in place of their formal charges |
| Charge: point charge coordinates and charges, list, add, update and delete, TIP3P conversion, export to a file | Prepare an external electrostatic field. The TIP3P conversion is checked as a candidate |
| Charge: whether external point charges go into the NWChem input, list of point charges (explains how to add them when there are none) | Set under “Environment” in “2 Calculation” (writes `bq units angstrom`) |
| Charge: electron count and consistency, whether input can be generated, input check | Shows the electron count with ECPs and ghosts taken into account, the α/β electron counts, errors and checks, and whether “Input can be generated”; Check in “4 Input preview” goes there |

Adding hydrogens first assigns aromatic bonds as single and double bonds (a Kekulé structure) and then counts valences. When a pyrrole- or imidazole-type nitrogen has lost its hydrogen, the hydrogen is put back on the nitrogen rather than on a carbon. Ions such as O²⁻, S⁻ and H⁻ are treated with valences that match their charge.

The charge settings cannot be changed while a candidate is shown or a cleanup is running. Applying the sum of formal charges does not guess the multiplicity. Placing a coordination center, placing or exchanging ligands and changing a geometry or oxidation number keep a total charge that matched the value the structure suggests (the sum of formal charges, with oxidation numbers for coordination centers) in step with the new value (placing Co(III) gives 3; two chlorides then bring it to 1). With coordination centers, the checks of “2 Calculation” and “Check structure…” compare the total charge with this value. External point charges are not added to the electron count or the total charge of the molecule. Writing point charges is off by default; enabling it is saved in the project and supports undo/redo. This is a fixed external electrostatic field, not an NWChem QM/MM calculation with a force field, topology and QM/MM boundary.

The counterpoise (BSSE) correction, distance, angle and dihedral constraints and coordinate scans that NWChem can run are set in “2 Calculation”. FMO and SAPT0 are not supported. Basis sets, ECPs and methods are also set in “2 Calculation”.

Old fragment assignments, constraints, scans and QM/MM sections of existing projects, as well as unknown fields, are kept when saving and are not converted to NWChem input. Loading never enables or removes old settings automatically. References are held as before, for example when atoms are deleted.

References: [NWChem coordinates, masses and ghosts](https://nwchemgit.github.io/Cartesian-coordinate-input.html), [fixed atoms](https://nwchemgit.github.io/Constraints.html), [geometry optimization](https://nwchemgit.github.io/Geometry-Optimization.html), [external point charges](https://nwchemgit.github.io/Bq.html), [counterpoise example](https://nwchemgit.github.io/Python.html).

The `editor` Qt Tests verify that the “Attributes” page has only atom attributes, ghosts and fixing (including where sorting, My parts, local cleanup, 3D regeneration and the review of held settings went), that fixing and point-charge operations in the UI reach the generated input, that the Charge page follows changes of the total charge, multiplicity and point-charge output setting made in “2 Calculation”, and they check the restrictions during candidates, undo/redo and save compatibility of old settings. Running `test_editor nwchemMoleculeControls` with `MOLFORMA_MOLECULE_ARTIFACTS=/tmp/molforma-molecule` saves screenshots of each page.

### Structure tools (Tools menu)

- **Point group and symmetrization**: “Tools → Point group and symmetrize…” shows the point group as a Schoenflies symbol (C1, Cs, Ci, Cn, Cnv, Cnh, Dn, Dnh, Dnd, S2n, T, Td, Th, O, Oh, I, Ih, C∞v, D∞h) within a given tolerance (default 0.05 Å), together with the largest deviation from the symmetric positions. Ghosts, isotopes and atoms with a specified mass count as separate kinds, and up to 200 atoms are analyzed. “Symmetrize (as candidate)” averages the coordinates over the symmetry operations found and makes the exactly symmetric structure a candidate. It is refused if a fixed atom would move. Because the point-group detection of Open Babel misidentifies water as Cs and methane as C3v, the search for and classification of symmetry operations are MolForma's own implementation. The NWChem input uses `noautosym`, so calculations do not use symmetry.
- **Z-matrix**: “Tools → Z-matrix (internal coordinates)…” shows internal coordinates in atom number order as a table (each atom references earlier atoms, preferring bonded atoms and avoiding collinear references). Editing a distance, angle or dihedral cell moves that atom's side (beyond the bond) and applies the change to the molecule; Undo reverts it. “Copy text” gives the text in `H 1 0.960000 2 104.5000` format. A Z-matrix cannot be read as input.
- **Regenerate 3D**: “Tools → Regenerate 3D” rebuilds the 3D coordinates from the bonds and shows the result, refined with a force field, as a candidate.
- **Conformer search**: “Tools → Conformer search…” rotates rotatable bonds at random, optimizes the conformers with MMFF94 (UFF where it cannot be used), removes duplicates with a heavy-atom RMSD below 0.25 Å and lists them by energy. The chosen conformer becomes a candidate (the most stable one when the search was started over MCP). If only conformers identical to the current structure are found (for example in molecules without rotatable bonds), no candidate is made and the status bar says so. It runs in a separate process and can be cancelled. Molecules with fixed atoms, metals or ghosts are not supported. This is a force-field conformer search and does not guarantee the most stable conformer at the quantum-chemical level.
- **Structure check**: “Tools → Check structure…” (the same as “Check structure…” on the “Residue” page of “1 Molecule”) checks the atom and bond counts, the formal charges against the charge setting, the combination of electron count and multiplicity, and the bond order sums. It also shows the number of open sites at coordination centers, and any difference between the charge setting and the total charge expected from the oxidation states (auxiliary) of coordination centers and the formal charges of ligands. If there are no problems, it shows “No problems found.”
- **Structure comparison and superposition**: “Tools → Compare and superpose structures…” compares the molecule with another structure file (the same atoms in the same order) and shows the RMSD of the coordinates as they are, the RMSD after Kabsch superposition (all atoms and non-hydrogen atoms) and the largest deviation. “Superpose onto it (candidate)” moves the whole current molecule to the position and orientation of the compared structure. “Compare” in the result viewer performs the same calculation.

### Window layout

- **Header**: The file name is at the left (click it for New, Open, Recent files, From SMILES and Save), the workflow in the middle (“1 Molecule” – “4 Input preview”, “Result viewer” and “History”), and “Undo”, “Redo” and “Run calculation” (F5) at the right (while a calculation runs, “Running” with the elapsed time and “Stop” replace “Run calculation”). The tabs “2 Calculation” – “4 Input preview” have no undo, redo or tab-switching buttons of their own; use those in the header. The workflow buttons switch tabs (as do Ctrl+1–Ctrl+6) and highlight the open tab in color. “4 Input preview” turns red when the input cannot be generated, with the reason in its tooltip. In a narrow window, the workflow moves to a second header row.
- **“1 Molecule”**: The tool column is at the left edge, with the parts palette to its right (collapse it with “Palette” below the tool column; collapsing widens the 3D view and the coordinate table, and the state is remembered in the app settings. It opens for the “Place” and “Replace” tools, for “Open ligand palette” and when saving to My parts), the 3D view in the middle, and the pages “Edit”, “Transform”, “Attributes”, “Coordination”, “Residue” and “Charge” on the right. The row above the 3D view shows the current tool's settings (“Select by”, “Bond type”, the part to place, “Keep tool after placing a part”, “Cap with hydrogens after deleting”, the charge to place) and “Auto-add hydrogens”, “Add hydrogens” and “Clean up all”. When there is a candidate, the same row switches to confirming or discarding it. The row height does not change with candidates or tools, so the 3D view keeps its size and position (except when you switch to a tool whose settings wrap to two lines in a narrow window).
- **Overlays on the 3D view**: The peptide sequence is at the top left (per chain in residue-number order, with terminal caps at the ends as in “ACE 0 · ALA 1 · … · NME 4”; click a residue to select its atoms; with 25 or more residues, the first 24 are shown), +X/+Y/+Z, “Fit to view”, “Rotate” and “View” at the top right, and the distance, angle or dihedral of 2–4 atoms selected in order, with an input to change the dimension, at the bottom left. When the 3D view is narrow, the top-right buttons show only icons and the sequence moves below them. The two lines below the 3D view show what a click does with the current tool and the mouse controls (what does not fit is cut off with “…”, and the tooltip shows the full text).
- **Display options**: “View” at the top right of the 3D view is the same as the “View” menu and toggles hydrogen display (H), numbers, labels, the display style, measurements and more. “Rotate” opens sliders for the left–right, up–down and in-plane rotation angles.

### Tools and parts palette

- **Tools**: Choose a tool in the column at the left edge (Select, Move, Bond, Delete, Place, Replace, Charge; each tool's key is shown in its corner), with “Edit → Choose tool”, or with keys in “1 Molecule” (S, M, B, X, A, R, Q; in input fields they type characters). The row above the 3D view shows the current tool's settings, and below the 3D view appear what a click does with the current tool and the name of the element or part to place. Clicking a part or atom in the palette switches to the “Place” tool. While the “Replace” tool is active, however, it stays and replaces with the chosen element or part. Going back to “Place” or “Replace” after the “Select” tool highlights and uses the palette item chosen before.
- **Place tool**: Places what you chose in the palette, depending on where you click. An atom goes into empty space or is joined to the clicked atom with a bond of the “Bond type” (clicking a hydrogen replaces that hydrogen; clicking a bond inserts the atom into it). An inserted atom goes where covalent radii and a tetrahedral angle put it (for example C–O 1.42 Å, C–O–C 109.5°), and the smaller fragment moves outward; in a ring, or with fixed atoms on both sides, nothing else moves and the atom sits off the bond line (on it the force field could not open the angle). Skeletons, amino acids and terminal caps go into empty space or attach to an atom or hydrogen. A substituent replaces the clicked hydrogen (it cannot be placed in empty space). A coordination center goes into empty space with its coordination sites; the element placed is the item highlighted in the palette (the first one right after a category change). A ligand coordinates to a purple number (open site) of a coordination center; clicking empty space places it as a separate molecule.
- **Bond tool**: Choose the bond type (“Single bond” to “Delete bond”) with “Bond type” above the 3D view (the same setting as “Bond type” on the “Edit” page; it is also shown for the “Place” tool, which uses it when joining an atom to an atom). Setting a bond to the type it already has makes no change and no history entry.
- **Move tool**: Dragging an atom moves it with its hydrogens (dragging a selected atom moves the whole selection and its hydrogens) along with the cursor, and releasing commits the move as one edit. If fixed atoms are involved, nothing moves and the reason is shown on release. Alt-drag rotates.
- **Placing ligands one after another**: With a ligand chosen in the “Place” tool, clicking the next open site confirms the candidate of the previous ligand and makes the next one a candidate.
- **When the tool switches back**: Placing one part such as a skeleton, substituent or amino acid (including a ligand placed on its own) returns to the “Select” tool and clears the palette highlight (turn on “Keep tool after placing a part” to keep placing). Placing atoms, coordination centers and ligands on open sites, and all other tools, continue until you switch.
- **Common elements**: The palette buttons H, C, N, O, F, P, S, Cl, Br and I make that element ready to place with the “Place” tool (the button of the chosen element is highlighted in color). The list of the “Atom” category also shows the chosen element and these elements.
- **Categories and layout**: The buttons at the top of the palette (All, Atom, Skeleton, Substituent, Coordination center, Ligand, Amino acid, My parts) switch the category. The element buttons (H, C, N, …) and “Periodic table…” choose the center element under “Coordination center” (non-metals such as S, P and Xe join the top of the list) and, from the part categories such as Skeleton or Substituent, switch to “Atom” and choose that element. “My parts” shows “Save selected atoms…”, “Load…” and “Remove from list” above the parts (removing a part from the list does not delete its file; without parts, it explains how to add some). Parts and atoms are laid out as tiles with a structure thumbnail and a name (up to two lines; the full name is in the tooltip); headings and the Build 3D from SMILES “…” item take a full row.
- **Order in “All”**: Without a search, atoms, skeletons, substituents, ligands, amino acids, terminal caps, coordination centers (metals) and My parts are listed in this order under headings.
- **Palette search**: An element symbol or atomic number puts that element at the top of the list (“N” gives nitrogen; under “Coordination center”, as a coordination center). Elements can also be found by their Japanese name (such as 窒素) or English name. Part names and SMILES find parts too, and when a string that matches no part can be read as SMILES, Build 3D from SMILES “…” appears (it makes a candidate placed beside the current molecule). Enter in the search field chooses the first item.
- **From SMILES**: An input field with examples makes a candidate that replaces the current molecule. If the SMILES cannot be read, the reason is shown, and “Edit SMILES…” lets you enter it again.

### Selection, deletion, candidates, view and keys

- **Selection**: Clicking an atom selects only that atom (or its group when “Select by” is Part, Residue, Molecule or All). Shift/Ctrl+click adds to or removes from the selection, and the selection order is used for measuring distances, angles and dihedrals and for changing dimensions. Shift/Ctrl-drag selects a region (Ctrl adds to the selection), Ctrl+A selects all and Esc clears the selection. “Select by” is chosen above the 3D view with the Select and Move tools, and the Delete and Replace tools use the same unit (when it is not Atom, it is shown in the description below the 3D view).
- **Selection box**: The top of the “Edit” page lists the selected atoms in selection order (residue atoms as “ALA 1 · CA”), up to 8 atoms, with “Deselect”, “Delete selected atoms”, “Local cleanup” (refines only the selected atoms with a force field and shows a candidate), “Keep selection after edits” (on by default) and “Cap with hydrogens after deleting”. Operations on the selected atoms (delete, fix, ghost, to origin, translate and so on) are only on the right-side pages; the selection bar of the coordinate table shows only the selected atoms and Deselect.
- **Operations on the selection**: Buttons that act on selected atoms (delete, bond, apply coordinates, dimensions, rotate and so on) stay disabled until the required number of atoms is selected, and their tooltips say how many to select. With no atoms selected, the coordinate and dimension fields on the right show “—” instead of keeping the values of previously selected atoms.
- **Deletion**: “Delete selected atoms” on the “Edit” page, the Delete key, the Delete tool and the context menu perform the same deletion and show it as a candidate. Hydrogens bonded only to the deleted atoms are removed as well, and when “Cap with hydrogens after deleting” is on (one setting shared by the selection box of the “Edit” page and the Delete tool; on by default), deleting heavy atoms adds hydrogens to the atoms whose bonds were cut (not when only hydrogens are deleted). When the total charge matched the sum of the formal charges (with the oxidation numbers of coordination centers, if any) before the deletion, it follows the new sum (deleting a Na⁺ coordination cluster takes it from +1 back to 0, for example). The Delete key in the coordinate table performs the same deletion.
- **Candidates**: Changes that are not yet confirmed are shown in amber in the row above the 3D view (where the tool settings are; it does not cover the view or change the size of the 3D view), with the operation name and the numbers of added, removed and moved atoms, using the same marks as the 3D view (added: green circle, removed: red × at the old position, moved: orange circle). The top of the “Edit” page lists up to 8 atoms to be removed or added. Ctrl+Enter confirms and Esc discards (from any tab). On tabs other than “1 Molecule”, the same bar appears below the tabs. The tooltips of “Undo” and “Redo” and the “Edit” menu show the name of the operation concerned.
- **Undo and redo**: Undo/redo is shared by the molecule and the settings of all tabs. When there is nothing to undo or redo, the buttons and menu items are disabled (“Confirm changes” is also enabled only while there is a candidate). Undoing or redoing shows the operation name in the status bar and, when it changes another tab than the current one (for example a basis set change undone from “1 Molecule”), adds the tab name as in (in “2 Calculation”).
- **View**: Left-drag rotates about the vertical and horizontal axes of the screen (trackball), with no rotation bias that depends on the orientation. A file with a three-dimensional structure opens viewed from an oblique direction, as in the result viewer (planar molecules and new molecules are viewed from the front). After an edit, the view is refitted only when the molecule extends beyond the view; otherwise the view does not move (so that the atom you click next stays in place). “+Z” shows the whole molecule viewed from the front (+Z direction), and “Fit to view” (F) fits the whole molecule without changing the orientation. The rotation sliders (“Rotate” at the top right of the 3D view) follow mouse operations and +X/+Y/+Z, and touching a slider does not make the view jump. The wheel zooms around the cursor position. The controls at the top right of the 3D view (+X/+Y/+Z, “Fit to view”, “Rotate”, “View”) are in the same order in the result viewer.
- **Labels**: “View → Labels” chooses the atoms that show their element symbol and number (All, Heavy atoms, Selected atoms, None), and “View → Show numbers” whether serial numbers are shown (the same choices are in “View” at the top right of the 3D view). Each label is placed at the upper right, lower right, upper left or lower left of its atom, wherever it does not overlap other labels or atoms. Labels that do not fit are dropped, keeping those of selected atoms, coordination centers, heavy atoms and atoms in front first. The result viewer has the same choices (saved separately).
- **Keys**: Delete (deletes the selected atoms; with no selection, the status bar says so), Ctrl+A (select all), Ctrl+C (copy the selected atoms, or the whole molecule, as XYZ), Ctrl+V (add the XYZ on the clipboard as a fragment beside the current molecule), 1/2/3 (bond order of 2 selected atoms), H (show hydrogens) and F (fit to view) work only while “1 Molecule” has the focus; in input fields they do what the field normally does. Ctrl+Enter, Esc, Ctrl+1–Ctrl+6 (switch tabs), Ctrl+Z/Y and so on work everywhere. “Help → Keyboard shortcuts” lists all of them with where they apply, and the mouse controls.
- **Center of rotation and mirroring**: “Rotate selection” and “Mirror in plane” on the “Transform” page use, by default, an axis or plane through the center of the selected atoms (the mean of their coordinates), so the molecule changes its orientation in place. Setting “Rotation/mirror center” to “Origin” uses the coordinate origin. “Make a mirrored copy” places the copy 6 Å from the original molecule in the +X direction, mirrored in place.
- **Response with large molecules**: Each edit, undo or selection updates only the visible tab and the coordinate table; a hidden coordinate table and the other tabs (“2 Calculation” – “4 Input preview”) are brought up to date when they are shown (and before MCP reads or operates the GUI). The red tab name and description of “4 Input preview” follow input errors even while it is hidden. On a 2160-atom water cluster, select all takes about 0.1 s, and editing coordinates or undoing about 0.1 s (previously about 13 s and about 1 s). Undo keeps up to the last 100 operations.
- **Colors**: The accent color of the selected tool, the open tab, “Run calculation”, the category buttons and so on is MolForma's own blue (#1D5FD1 in light themes, #2F6FE4 in dark themes) and does not follow the desktop selection color. The backgrounds, text and lines of the header, the tool column and the overlays on the 3D view, the candidate banner, error text and the coloring of the input preview are derived from the Qt palette so that they stay readable in dark themes. The candidate banner is pale amber in light themes and dark amber in dark themes. The background of the 3D view stays light (only when nothing is shown does it take the window color in dark themes).

## Atom palette and periodic table

When the palette category in “1 Molecule” is “Atom” or “All”, “Periodic table…” opens a Qt periodic table dialog. Click an element among the 18 groups and 7 periods, or in the lanthanide/actinide rows below. You can search by Japanese name, English name, element symbol or atomic number; cells that do not match keep their position and are dimmed. The arrow keys move between cells, Enter/Space chooses and Esc cancels. When the search matches a single element, Enter in the search field also chooses it.

The chosen element is shown in the palette with its colored symbol, its name and its atomic number, and can be placed by clicking or dragging in the molecule view. Choosing in the periodic table alone does not change the molecule, and placing supports undo/redo. The added elements Fr–Og are handled throughout: changing elements, coordinate input, saving projects, XYZ, NWChem coordinate output, and reading structures from NWChem logs, CUBE and MOLDEN files.

The elements covered are H–Og (atomic numbers 1–118), which have official names in the [NWChem element table](https://github.com/nwchemgit/nwchem/blob/master/src/geom/periodic.fh). The provisional elements Uue and Ubn (119, 120) are not covered. **Recognizing an element is separate from basis set support.** If the molecule contains elements that the built-in basis sets of “2 Calculation” do not support (such as Fr–Og), generating the calculation input is refused as before. Input of arbitrary basis sets or ECPs that are not in the library is not implemented.

H–Rn use the bundled element data (properties, isotopes); the masses, covalent radii, colors and English names of Fr–Og come from the element data of Open Babel 3.2.1. The radii of heavy elements include estimated and substitute values, so bond perception and placement are approximate. The masses are database reference values; standard atomic weights of superheavy elements and force-field support for all elements are not guaranteed.

## Coordinate table (“1 Molecule”)

- A table of atom coordinates and attributes, shown below the 3D view of “1 Molecule” (or in its place). Switch the layout with “3D”, “3D + table” and “Table” at the right end of the strip below the 3D view, or with “View → 1 Molecule layout” (also available from the other tabs; it opens “1 Molecule”). The layout is remembered in the app settings (default “3D”). In “3D + table”, drag the border to change the heights (initially 3D:table = 6:4; the dragged position is remembered in the app settings). The table keeps at least 4 rows (also when the operation row above it wraps in a narrow width), and in a narrow window the 3D view shrinks instead. Numbers in the coordinate table are shown right-aligned with 6 decimal places; cells being edited and the saved values keep their full precision.
- The “Atom” column of the table shows the same numbered name as the 3D view (`C14`) in the element's color; fixed atoms have a lock mark and dimmed coordinates. Editing the cell changes the element symbol. Atoms that belong to residues, such as in peptides, show `GLY 2 · CA` in the “Residue · name” column. A blank mass shows “Default”. The column numbers of the table (`column` of MCP's `ui_edit_cell`) are as before: 0 element, 1–3 X/Y/Z, 4 mass, 5 ghost, 6 fixed, then 7 residue, 8 candidate change, and the attribute columns 9 formal charge, 10 unpaired electrons, 11 atom name.
- The filter field in the operation row shows only the rows whose numbered atom name, element or residue name contains the text. The filter affects only the display; it does not change what selection, output and editing apply to. Below the table, the numbers of atoms, selected atoms and fixed atoms (and shown rows while filtering) are displayed.
- The selection bar shows the selected atoms (up to 3 atoms, with their residues for up to 2) and Deselect (×); only in the “Table” layout (without the 3D view) does it also show the distance, angle or dihedral when 2–4 atoms are selected (with the 3D view, they appear in its measurement box). Fixing, ghosting, deleting and moving selected atoms to the origin are done on the right-side pages beside the table (“Edit”, “Transform”, “Attributes”). The “Ghost” and “Fixed” columns of the table can be toggled directly per row. The Delete key in the table is the same as deletion in “1 Molecule” (it follows the “Cap with hydrogens after deleting” setting and shows a candidate). While the table is visible (“3D + table”, “Table”), “Coordinates (one atom)” on “Edit” is hidden (you edit the same values in the table cells).
- When there is a candidate, an amber bar appears in place of the selection bar and the table shows the coordinates of the candidate read-only. The “Change” column marks the atoms added or moved relative to the current structure, and “Changed” filters the table to those rows. Atoms are compared by ID. However, for replacements in which all atoms get new IDs, such as loading a structure or pasting XYZ, when one element sequence matches the start of the other (reloading the same structure, appending atoms at the end), atoms are compared by their order, and differences below 0.001 Å (the 3-decimal precision of PDB) count as the same position. This comparison is also used for the marks in the 3D view of “1 Molecule” and for the added, removed and moved counts in the candidate bar.
- With no atoms, “Import structure…”, “Paste XYZ…” and “Add atom…” are shown instead of the table. Dropping an XYZ, MOL, SDF, PDB or ENT file on the table creates a replacement candidate, like “Import structure…” (other files, and drops on the 3D view or other tabs, open in the window as before).
- Cells are edited individually: element, X/Y/Z, mass number or amu, ghost, fixed, and the attribute columns (formal charge −99 to 20, unpaired electrons 0 to 8, atom name). The element accepts the 118 elements H–Og plus D/T, and input such as `@C`. Changing the element clears a mass set for the old element. NaN, infinity, unknown elements and duplicate atoms are refused with an error, and the cell reverts to its value. A failed edit reaches neither the history nor the molecule.
- The display units are Å/Bohr (1 Bohr = 0.529177210903 Å). Switching units or moving the camera does not change physical coordinates. Values are shown with 14 significant digits, and only the edited cell is read back, so the precision of the other coordinates is preserved.
- The add-atom dialog takes one atom in the same units as the table. Choose the element with the buttons for common elements (H, C, N, O, F, P, S, Cl, Br, I, D, T), from the list or with “Periodic table…”; entering X/Y/Z shows the nearest atom and its distance. Bonds and hydrogens are not added automatically. The “Structure” menu in the operation row has “Import structure file…”, “Replace with pasted XYZ…”, “Sort atoms” (By element, By residue, Selected atoms first; this changes the order in the table and in the NWChem coordinates, and can be undone) and “Delete all atoms”. Deleting selected atoms, deleting all atoms, importing a structure file and pasting XYZ are shown as candidates to confirm or discard. Changing the coordinates of fixed atoms, deleting them and replacing the structure are refused.
- To move one atom to the origin, use “Align (origin, +X, XY)” on “Transform” (in the “3D” layout, “Move atom to origin” in “Coordinates (one atom)” does the same). It translates the whole molecule and the point charges, and is refused if a fixed atom would move.
- XYZ / MOL / SDF / Mol2 / CML / PDB / ENT can be read as structures (Mol2 and CML through Open Babel). For PDB, choose MODEL/ALTLOC; for multi-frame XYZ (such as optimization trajectories), choose the frame to load (default: the last one). NWChem input (`.nw`) and Gaussian input (`.gjf` / `.com`) can also be opened ([Importing NWChem and Gaussian input](#importing-nwchem-and-gaussian-input)). Export formats are XYZ, SDF, MOL, Mol2, CML, PDB and Gaussian input. Replacing the structure by loading or pasting keeps the run and result information and the calculation settings of the project, and settings that reference old atoms are held. Bonds are taken over from the input structure. For XYZ, bonds are perceived from distances, and Open Babel assigns double, triple and aromatic bond orders from the geometry. XYZ has no charges, so for ions set the formal charges before adding hydrogens. Bonds to metals and ghosts stay single bonds. “File → Replace with pasted XYZ”, “From SMILES” and “Replace the existing molecule” in “Build from sequence” also replace only the structure and keep the calculation settings, run settings and project information. XYZ input and output are in Å regardless of the table's display unit. `@element` and a mass in the fifth column are MolForma extensions that general XYZ readers may not support. To save fixing, bonds, residues, point charges and the like, use the project format.

### NWChem output

The “Export” menu in the operation row copies the NWChem geometry block or XYZ (always Å), or writes it to a file (“Export …”, `.nw` / `.xyz`). The unit of the NWChem coordinates is independent of the display unit of the table and is set with “Coordinate units” in “2 Calculation” (saved in `document.nwchem.geometry_units`, with undo/redo support); the menu item names show the current unit. The old-format setting `job.bohr` is not reused as the NWChem unit.

- Writes `geometry units angstrom` or `geometry units au`. Å output states `angstrom_to_au` explicitly so that the conversion matches Bohr output.
- `nocenter noautosym` creates the input with the given atom order and coordinate frame. Geometry optimizations and transition state searches without fixed atoms, ghosts or internal-coordinate constraints omit `noautoz` and optimize in NWChem's automatic internal coordinates (autoz). In B3LYP/6-31G* tests, they converged to the same minimum as Cartesian coordinates in fewer iterations (benzoic acid 29→12, ethanol 12→5, glycine 21→16, biphenyl 22→10, water dimer 34→26). When internal coordinates cannot be built, as for linear molecules, NWChem switches to Cartesian coordinates automatically. For weak complexes that converge poorly and similar cases, you can switch with “Optimize in Cartesian coordinates (noautoz)” in the advanced settings of “Optimization and frequencies” in “2 Calculation”. All other calculations (single-point energy, frequencies only, IRC, etc.) and optimizations with fixed atoms or ghosts use Cartesian coordinates with `noautoz` (because NWChem cannot handle Cartesian fixing and internal coordinates together). When internal-coordinate constraints or relaxed scans are set in “2 Calculation”, `zcoord` is written to handle the constraints.
- Coordinates, masses and point charges are written in the shortest notation that reads back as the same double value (`0.758`, not a 17-digit form such as `0.75800000000000001`). NWChem's automatic symmetrization and editing periodic boundary conditions are not supported (to symmetrize a structure, use “Tools → Point group and symmetrize”).
- Isotope mass numbers are converted to amu with the isotope data of Open Babel C++ and written as NWChem `mass`. Mass numbers are never written as actual masses. Arbitrary masses can be given as decimals; isotopes that are not in the database need an explicit mass.
- Ghosts are written as centers with zero nuclear charge, such as `bqC`. `basis` needs an explicit basis assignment such as `bqC library C <basis-name>`, and a note on this is added at the end of the exported fragment. Formal charges of atoms are not turned into NWChem nuclear charge specifications.
- Fixed flags are converted to `fix atom` in `constraints` with the atom numbers at export. In geometry optimizations, ghost centers are fixed as well: an unfixed ghost, which has no nucleus, is pulled toward real atoms together with its basis functions, and the optimization does not converge. The leading `clear` removes earlier fixing conditions, so when you insert this into existing input, add the other constraints you need to the same block.

The export from the table is an **input fragment of the coordinates and fixed atoms**. Create the complete calculation input in “2 Calculation” and check it in “4 Input preview”. Export is disabled while there is an unconfirmed candidate. Calculations are run with “3 Run settings”, and output is viewed in the “Result viewer”. Existing NWChem input can be imported with “File → Open”. Using the table does not require an NWChem installation.

Input specification references: [Cartesian coordinate input](https://nwchemgit.github.io/Cartesian-coordinate-input.html), [Geometry keywords](https://nwchemgit.github.io/Keywords-for-the-GEOMETRY-directive.html), [Constraints](https://nwchemgit.github.io/Constraints.html), [Basis sets](https://nwchemgit.github.io/Basis.html).

The element coverage of the basis sets is checked against a fixed list built from the per-element entries of the NWChem 7.3.1 library files. The basis set files themselves are not bundled. STO-3G covers H–I, 3-21G H–Cs, 6-31G/6-31G** H–Zn, 6-31G* H–Kr, 6-31+G*, 6-31++G**, 6-311+G** and 6-311++G** H–Ca, and 6-311G** H–Ca, Ga–Kr and I. cc-pVDZ/TZ/QZ cover H–Kr (except K), aug-cc-pVDZ/TZ H–Kr (except K and Ca), the def2 family H–La and Hf–Rn, and LANL2DZ H, Li–La, Hf–Bi and U–Pu. The selected library must be available where the calculation runs.

In the “Per-element basis” table you can choose a different basis set for each element in the molecule (for example 6-31G* for the organic ligands and def2-TZVP or LANL2DZ for the metal only). When such assignments exist, `*` is not used and a line such as `Fe library "def2-tzvp"` is written for each element (NWChem treats a second library for the same element as an addition, not a replacement). Ghosts get the basis set of their element. Assignments for elements not in the molecule are kept but not used in the output. `basis spherical / cartesian` applies to all elements, so a warning is shown when the per-element basis sets differ in their d-function convention. Custom basis sets or ECPs that are not in the library are not supported.

“Auto” for “d/f functions” writes 6-31G, 6-31G* and 6-31G** as Cartesian (6d), matching the definitions of these basis sets, and all others as spherical harmonics (5d, 7f) (the same convention as Gaussian's defaults). The spherical-harmonics setting of earlier projects and user presets is carried over as an explicit choice. A warning is shown when you explicitly choose a type that differs from the convention. Use “Auto” when comparing with literature values.

For the def2 family, `def2-ecp` is assigned automatically, removing 28 electrons for Rb–Xe, 46 for Cs–La and 60 for Hf–Rn; for LANL2DZ, `lanl2dz ecp` is assigned, removing 10 electrons for Na–Cu, 18 for Zn, 28 for Ga–Ag, 36 for Cd, 46 for In–La, 60 for Hf–Au, 68 for Hg and Tl, and 78 for Pb, Bi and U–Pu (the `nelec` of the NWChem ECP library). ECPs are attached to real atoms only, and the electron count is corrected before the α/β electron counts and the multiplicity are checked for consistency. Ghosts have a nuclear charge of 0 and get an explicit basis set such as `bqC library C ...`. `freeze atomic` for correlated calculations is off by default. When it is on, the number of orbitals frozen by NWChem's per-atom rules is shown, and settings that leave too few correlated electrons are rejected. ECP centers and ghosts are excluded from the frozen core.

Dispersion corrections can be chosen only for the combinations for which NWChem 7.3.1 writes Grimme's published parameters. D3(BJ) for the M06 family and D3 for SCAN and r²SCAN are rejected because NWChem has no parameters for them, and D3(BJ) for B3PW91 and D3 for PW6B95 because NWChem's values do not match the published ones. ωB97X-D3 already includes D3 and is used without a correction. “Custom xc” takes the `xc` line of the DFT block as one line of letters, digits, `_`, `.`, `+`, `-` and spaces (for example `xpbe96 0.75 cpbe96 1.0 hfexch 0.25`). NWChem checks the names at run time, and no dispersion correction can be chosen. On 2026-10-24, inputs with every added functional and every allowed dispersion correction were confirmed to finish normally with NWChem 7.3.1.

Frequencies use an analytic HF/DFT Hessian by default, and a numerical Hessian for MP2, ROHF, ECPs, COSMO, and DFT with meta-GGA, range-separated or custom xc functionals (NWChem has no analytic Hessian for meta-GGA and range-separated functionals and switches to finite differences automatically). You can also choose the numerical Hessian manually. Frequency analysis with fixed atoms, ghosts or external point charges, and COSMO with ghosts, are not supported. Because the coordinates are written with `noautosym`, NWChem computes the thermochemistry with a rotational symmetry number σ=1. For molecules of up to 60 atoms without isotopes or mass assignments, MolForma estimates σ by counting the rotations that map the molecule onto itself as a permutation of its atoms; if σ>1, a warning shows the corrections to the entropy (−R ln σ) and the free energy (+RT ln σ, 298.15 K). When frequencies follow an optimization, the two `task` lines are written in order. SCF convergence is 1e-5 for Normal, 1e-7 for Tight (default) and 1e-9 for Very tight, applied to `thresh` for HF and to energy/density/gradient for DFT. “NWChem default” writes none of these and uses NWChem's default thresholds (a warning is shown because they can be too loose for frequency calculations). The CC convergence threshold is 1e-7. Individual thresholds set in the advanced settings of “SCF and correlation” override the corresponding values.

### Saving, presets and compatibility

- NWChem-specific settings are saved in `document.nwchem`. `geometry_units` is shared with the export of the coordinate table. The total charge and multiplicity share the existing `job.charge` / `job.multiplicity`, so changes made in “1 Molecule” are reflected too. The formal charges of the atoms and the total charge of the calculation are kept apart, and a warning is shown when they differ.
- NWChem settings are not inferred from the method, basis set or detail sections of an old-format `job`. Projects without NWChem settings show HF/STO-3G as the initial settings. Old-format settings, unknown fields, and run and result references are kept when saving. Old-format constraints, scans, FMO/CP/SAPT, relativistic settings, occupation numbers and the like are not converted; if such advanced settings exist, the screen points this out. Fixed-atom flags are written to NWChem.
- Point charges are not written by default. When you enable them on this tab or in “1 Molecule → Charge”, they are written to `bq units angstrom`. This is independent of the old-format QM/MM flag. Point-charge coordinates are written explicitly in Å, regardless of the molecule's output units.
- Built-in presets are HF/STO-3G, B3LYP-D3(BJ)/def2-SVP optimization + frequencies, and frozen-core MP2/cc-pVDZ. Applying a preset can be reverted with a single undo. The tooltip of the description field lists the changes before you apply it. Presets do not change the molecule, charge, multiplicity, calculation name, title, memory, units or unknown fields. All other basic settings, including the reference, solvent model, dielectric constant and the output of external point charges, are replaced by the preset's values. Saved and removed user presets are stored in the local QSettings.
- The title and calculation name take effect when the entry is committed. The characters and length of the calculation name and line breaks, quotes and the like in the title are checked so that no other NWChem directive can slip in. The input preview is read-only.

Turning on “Excited states (TDDFT)” writes a `tddft` block (`nroots`, `notriplet` for closed shells when triplets are not requested, and `cis` for the Tamm–Dancoff approximation) and `task tddft energy`. Open shells (UKS) give unrestricted excited states without the singlet/triplet separation. TDDFT in NWChem 7.3.1 stops with meta-GGA functionals (TPSS, SCAN, r²SCAN, the M06 family, PW6B95), so they are rejected; geometry optimization with TDDFT (excited-state gradients) is not covered either. Combinations with CAM-B3LYP, ωB97X-D3, COSMO and dispersion corrections have been tested in real runs.

Running calculations and MPI launch settings are in “3 Run settings”; output is viewed in the “Result viewer”. TCE, MD, periodic systems and QM/MM are out of scope (the directives they need can be written in the additional input). You can edit settings and generate input without an NWChem installation. The input check does not guarantee convergence or the validity of the physical model.

### Solvents, relativity and RI-J

- The solvent models are “COSMO” and “SMD (DFT, named solvent)”. For the solvent, choose “Dielectric constant (COSMO only)” or one of water, methanol, ethanol, 1-propanol, 1-butanol, acetonitrile, acetone, DMSO, DMF, THF, 1,4-dioxane, diethyl ether, ethyl acetate, dichloromethane, chloroform, nitromethane, pyridine, benzene, toluene, n-hexane and cyclohexane; the NWChem SMD solvent code (`h2o`, `acetntrl`, `dcm` and so on) is written to the `cosmo` block as `solvent`. With a named solvent, COSMO also uses the dielectric constant from the same table. SMD adds `do_cosmo_smd true` and gives the solvation free energy including the non-electrostatic (CDS) terms. SMD in NWChem 7.3.1 is DFT-only, so combining it with HF is rejected (a real run stops with “cosmo-smd can be used only with dft and sodft”). Frequencies in solvent use a numerical Hessian.
- Relativistic Hamiltonians are written to the `relativistic` block as `zora on`, `douglas-kroll dkh` or `x2c on`. NWChem's ZORA is DFT-only, so choose DKH or X2C for HF (a real run stops with “ZORA available only for DFT”). Combining them with an ECP is rejected because the core would be treated twice, and a warning is shown when a non-relativistic basis set is used for Kr and heavier elements. ZORA total energies cannot be compared directly with non-relativistic, DKH or X2C energies. Frequencies with a relativistic Hamiltonian use a numerical Hessian.
- RI-J (DFT) writes `weigend coulomb fitting` (H–La, Hf–Rn; the same basis set on ghost centers) to `basis "cd basis"` and approximates the Coulomb term. Total energies differ slightly from a regular calculation, and frequencies use a numerical Hessian.

### Transition states, IRC, constraints and scans

- “Transition state search” is DRIVER's `task … saddle`. Start from a structure with negative curvature along the reaction coordinate, and check with “Transition state search → frequencies” that there is exactly one imaginary frequency.
- “IRC (reaction path from a transition state)” uses NWChem's Gonzalez–Schlegel method (the `mepgs` block and `task … mepgs`): it first computes the Hessian of the transition state, then follows the path both forward and backward up to “IRC points (per direction)” (`maxmep`). The step size (`stride`) is in mass-weighted coordinates, amu½·Bohr. If the points run out before a minimum is reached, NWChem ends with an error, so specify the number of points with a margin. NWChem 7.3.1 does not judge a minimum reached until the path length (stotal) exceeds 1 amu½·Bohr, so on a side where the minimum is close to the transition state it keeps taking small steps after reaching it, stops at the point limit with “mepgs: could not optimize”, and the other direction is not computed (the points so far can be seen in “Path / scan”). Then compute the remaining direction alone with “IRC direction” (both, forward only, backward only; `forward` / `backward` in `mepgs`). It cannot be used for structures with fixed atoms or ghosts.
- “Coordinate constraints” (optimization) optimizes with the distances, angles and dihedrals of 2–4 atoms selected in order in “1 Molecule” held at the values in the table (they are written as `bond`, `angle` and `torsion` with `constant` in `zcoord`, and `noautoz` is then dropped). The dihedral sign follows the same definition as MolForma's measurements. NWChem cannot handle internal coordinates together with fixed atoms (Cartesian constraints) (it stops with “cartesian constraints+internals”), so constraints cannot be combined with fixed atoms or ghosts. Deleting a constrained atom also deletes its constraint.
- “Coordinate scan” computes the distance, angle or dihedral chosen with “Use selected atoms” at evenly spaced points (2–50) from the start value to the end value. Each point is a named geometry (`geometry scanN`) that starts from coordinates in which the side of the atoms selected last has been moved. A relaxed scan (“Optimize the other coordinates at each point (relaxed scan)”) holds the scan coordinate fixed with `zcoord` and optimizes the rest; a rigid scan computes single-point energies. The `title` of each point reads `MolForma scan k/n`, and the Result viewer reads the energy and structure of each point from it. Relaxed scans cannot be combined with fixed atoms or ghosts either.

### Properties, BSSE and follow-up single point

- Of the “Properties”, Mulliken charges, NMR shielding constants (GIAO, `shielding`) and the static polarizability (`aoresponse 1 0.0`) are computed with one `property` block and `task … property`, and ESP and RESP charges with `esp` (`restrain`) and `task esp`. With a follow-up single point they use its settings. HF and DFT only. NMR and polarizability cannot be used with ROHF. When they are combined with MOLDEN output, `unset prop:*` is written before the `property` for MOLDEN so that NMR and the like are not computed twice.
- “BSSE correction” treats bonded molecules as monomers and writes a `bsse` block (`mon`, the sum of each monomer's formal charges as `charge`, `mult 1`). NWChem's BSSE module turns the partner into ghosts in each monomer calculation, so `bqX library X …` basis sets for all elements (also in `cd basis` with RI-J) are added automatically (without them it stops with “no basis on ghost atom”). It can be used for single-point energies, gradients and geometry optimizations, and only for singlets made of closed-shell monomers. It is rejected when the total charge differs from the sum of the formal charges, and in combination with solvent, TDDFT, properties, follow-up calculations, CUBE/MOLDEN or external point charges.
- The “BSSE error” and “Corrected energy” NWChem 7.3.1 prints add the errors of only the first half of the monomers (`task_bsse.F` sums the 2·nmon monomer energies with `do j = 1, nmon, 2`; with two monomers, only the first). When reading results, MolForma recomputes the correction over all the monomers from their energies after the supermolecule's (own basis, then the full one); when it differs from NWChem's, it warns and uses the recomputed correction and corrected energy (also for “Energy of the calculation” and ΔE in “History”). From the sum of the monomers in their own bases, the “Properties” tab also shows the interaction energy (corrected and uncorrected).
- “Follow-up single point” computes a single-point energy after an optimization or transition state search with another method, functional, dispersion correction or basis set (common to all elements). NWChem keeps settings in the runtime database, so `unset scf:*` and `unset dft:*` (`unset mp2:*` and `unset ccsd:*` for correlated calculations) are written before the follow-up calculation so that the CAM parameters and dispersion correction of the first settings do not carry over. Solvent, relativistic Hamiltonian and RI-J are used as they are. If the main calculation uses an ECP and the follow-up does not, it is rejected because the ECP would remain. The follow-up settings are checked by the same rules, and their errors are shown prefixed with “Follow-up single point: ”.

### Additional input (custom keywords)

In “Additional input (expert)”, “Before tasks” adds NWChem directives that MolForma does not handle (for example `set cphf:maxiter 50`, `task dft property`) verbatim just before the task line, and “End of input” adds them at the very end of the input. The content is not checked, so verify it in the input preview. `task shell` and `python` blocks, which can launch external programs, are rejected. Like the executable, launcher and environment variables, the additional input cannot be changed over MCP, and calculations started over MCP run only with the additional input set by the user. It is not included in presets.

### Importing NWChem and Gaussian input

Opening a `.nw` file with “File → Open…” imports the first Cartesian `geometry` (`units`, `bq` ghosts, `mass`, continuation lines with `\` and `;` separators), `charge`, the multiplicity and reference from `scf`/`dft`, `xc` (custom xc if it does not match a listed functional), dispersion correction and grid, `basis` (library names in the list, per element, RI-J through `cd basis`, spherical/cartesian), `cosmo` (dielectric constant, solvent name, SMD), `relativistic`, `tddft`, the `driver` iteration count, `property`, `esp`, `bsse`, `mepgs`, `fix atom` in `constraints`, and the `task` combination (optimization + frequencies, transition state search, IRC and so on). Calculation settings after the first task (follow-up calculations), the second and later geometries, `zcoord` and other blocks are not imported and are listed. Z-matrix geometries are rejected. The run settings use the defaults from Preferences.

Gaussian input (`.gjf` / `.com`) skips Link0, the route and the title, and imports the charge, multiplicity and Cartesian coordinates (supporting `C(Fragment=1)`, atomic numbers and the freeze-flag column). Inputs that use a Z-matrix or variables are rejected. The method and basis set of the route are not imported.

#### Scope and precedence

- Explicit SCF/DFT/CC thresholds, frozen orbital counts and optimization thresholds take precedence over the basic settings. For DRIVER, the individual thresholds are written after the preset directive such as Normal or Tight. A frozen orbital count of 0 turns off the frozen core, and counts that leave too few correlated electrons are rejected.
- Values that do not apply to the current calculation, for example DFT values kept after switching to HF, become “Pending” and are not written to the input. They are applied again when you switch back to a method or task that uses them. The finite-difference step of the numerical Hessian and the accuracy of the analytic Hessian switch by the same rule.
- Optimization displacements, the trust radius and the finite-difference step are in Bohr, gradients in Hartree/Bohr and energies in Hartree. They do not change when the coordinates are switched between Å and Bohr. Temperatures are given in K.
- Optimized structures use `<calculation name>_opt` and vibrational modes `<calculation name>_vib` as the file name prefix. The XYZ files are generated when NWChem itself runs the calculation.
- Input movecs name a file where the calculation runs; MolForma does not read it. Compatibility with the atom order and basis set has to be checked at run time. When you use existing movecs, specify a different output movecs file to avoid NWChem's default behavior of reusing the input file as the output. Identity through path aliases or symbolic links is not checked.
- movecs paths are quoted, so paths with spaces work. NWChem's startup directory settings use a different syntax, so `scratch_dir` / `permanent_dir` are written without quotes, and paths containing spaces, colons or quotes are rejected. Each directory must exist where the calculation runs. “3 Run settings” creates relative directories inside the job. Environment variable expansion, per-host or per-MPI-rank locations and multiple directories are not supported.
- While a candidate is shown or a cleanup is running, editing and output are disabled. Changing advanced settings never moves the molecule's coordinates or fixed atoms.

#### Saving advanced settings and compatibility

Only explicit settings are saved in `document.nwchem.advanced`. Merely opening the advanced settings does not add default values to the project. Unknown advanced fields are kept, reported as unsupported and excluded from the output. The old-format `job.sections` and the project's run and result references are kept as well.

User presets include the advanced settings except “Orbitals and files” and “CUBE / MOLDEN output”. When a built-in or old-format preset has no advanced settings, applying it clears these overrides so that the preset's basic settings take effect. The orbital, storage location, echo and visualization file output settings and unknown fields are kept, and the whole preset application can be reverted with a single undo.

The advanced settings do not offer custom keyword input, fine cache tuning of semi-direct SCF, RODFT/CDFT and fractional occupations, arbitrary orbital occupations, TCE, relativistic Hamiltonians, distance and angle constraints, scans, transition states, IRC, MD, periodic systems and the like. Old-format settings are not converted to these NWChem directives automatically. Neither NWChem nor Python is needed to edit or save advanced settings or to generate input.

References: [HF](https://nwchemgit.github.io/Hartree-Fock-Theory-for-Molecules.html), [DFT](https://nwchemgit.github.io/Density-Functional-Theory-for-Molecules.html), [MP2](https://nwchemgit.github.io/MP2.html), [CCSD](https://nwchemgit.github.io/CCSD.html), [DRIVER](https://nwchemgit.github.io/Geometry-Optimization.html), [Hessians and frequencies](https://nwchemgit.github.io/Hessians-and-Vibrational-Frequencies.html), [COSMO](https://nwchemgit.github.io/COSMO-Solvation-Model.html), [Scratch directory](https://nwchemgit.github.io/Scratch_Dir.html).

### Writing CUBE and MOLDEN files from the GUI

1. In the left list of “2 Calculation”, select “Orbitals and output files”. The “CUBE / MOLDEN output” settings are shown from the start.
2. Turn on “Write CUBE file”, “Write MOLDEN file” or both. Both can be written at the same time.
3. For CUBE, choose from “Total density (Alpha + Beta)”, “Alpha density”, “Beta density”, “Spin density (Alpha − Beta)” and “Molecular orbitals”. For a molecular orbital, choose Alpha/Beta and HOMO/LUMO/a number. Numbers start at 1. Beta orbitals are used with UHF/UKS.
4. Check the post-calculation `property` and `dplot` directives in “4 Input preview”, then run the calculation with “Run calculation” (F5) in the header.
5. When it finishes, open the job with “View results” in the run panel (“Calculation → Run status and log”) and choose the CUBE or MOLDEN file in the file list; it is loaded and displayed automatically.

The CUBE grid covers the minimum and maximum coordinates of the input structure along each axis plus 3 Å of padding, with the same grid spacing on all axes. By default the longest edge is divided into 40 intervals (41 points), and the other axes extend from the center with the same spacing to cover their range (no grid points are wasted for elongated molecules). You can change the padding and the number of divisions, or give “minimum maximum divisions” for each of X/Y/Z. The range is always in Å, independent of the display units of the coordinate table and the output units of the NWChem geometry. The grid is limited to 2 million points, and an invalid range, number of divisions or file name blocks writing the input. The grid range stays as determined from the input structure even after an optimization, so allow enough padding.

MOLDEN writes `<calculation name>.molden`, and CUBE writes `<calculation name>_<content/orbital number>.cube`. The CUBE name can be changed to a plain file name with the extension `.cube` or `.cub`. Both are saved in the `permanent_dir` of “Orbitals and files”, or directly in the job folder if it is not set. Saving the input set also records the output location in `run.json`, so the Result viewer lists files in the permanent directory too. Before a local run, conflicts with existing output are checked.

For the MOLDEN basis normalization, choose “NWChem convention” (default) or “Unnormalized (NONE)”. The orbitals and densities written are those of the HF/KS reference wavefunction; MP2/CCSD correlated densities and natural orbitals are not supported. After a frequency calculation, the HF/DFT energy is recomputed at the reference structure and its orbitals are written. HOMO/LUMO numbers are determined from the electron count, multiplicity, ECPs and spin; fractional occupations and excited states are not handled. Specifying an orbital number that does not exist (for example with a minimal basis set) causes an error when NWChem runs. CUBE output is one file with one content per calculation, and GUI output for structures containing ghost centers is not supported.

The settings are saved in the project and support the shared undo/redo, resets and reuse from History. When output is turned off, the grid, orbital and other settings are kept as pending and used again when it is turned back on. Applying a calculation preset keeps the output settings. Opening a MOLDEN file shows molecular orbitals from the basis set and MO coefficients. Molecular orbital CUBE files can be used as well.

References: [NWChem DPLOT](https://nwchemgit.github.io/DPLOT.html), [Properties / MOLDENFILE](https://nwchemgit.github.io/Properties.html#moldenfile).

## “3 Run settings”

Sets how NWChem and MPI are launched and saves the run as a job. At the top are the target calculation (one line with method, basis set and task), “Load into result viewer when done” and “Save as job”; on the left the settings (“Executable and job folder”, “Resources”, “Additional environment variables”); on the right the pre-run check and “Launch command”. - **Resources**: Shows the logical CPUs used (from processes and threads) as squares, one per logical CPU of this computer (red when exceeded), and the estimated total NWChem memory (processes × memory per process).
- **Run panel (run status and log)**: Shown on the right side of the window over the tabs (in “1 Molecule” it covers the position of the right-hand pages without hiding the 3D view, the controls above it or the layout switch; in this tab it starts below the row with the target calculation, “Load into result viewer when done” and “Save as job”), and opens when a calculation starts. You can open it from any tab with “Running” in the header, the run status in the status bar, “Calculation → Run status and log” (Ctrl+Shift+L), “Show run status and log” in “History” or “Show run status and log” in this tab; closing it does not stop the calculation. “Run status” shows the state of the saved or run job (Running, Finished, Stopped, Failed, Saved only), the elapsed time (plus the start time while running), the job folder, “Open folder”, “View results”, “Show in history” and, while running, “Stop…” (which asks for confirmation, like “Stop” in the header). While running, it shows each `task` of the input (geometry optimization, frequencies, etc.) as done, running or waiting; during an optimization it shows the latest step, energy and ΔE from the “@” lines of the log, and whether Gmax, Grms, Xmax and Xrms meet NWChem's thresholds. Below that is the log (stdout and stderr). The log of the running job is only read; the files are not modified.
- **Editing during a run**: You can edit the molecule and settings while a calculation runs; the changes do not affect a calculation already started (this tab says so too).

| Item | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| NWChem executable | Default `nwchem`. Before a local run, the file and its execute permission are checked |
| Launch mode | MPI or direct. MPI runs `mpirun/mpiexec -np <processes> <NWChem> input.nw` |
| MPI launcher, MPI processes | Default `mpirun` with 2 processes. Give the process count including MPI-PR helper processes; it is not adjusted automatically |
| OpenMP / BLAS | Threads per process. Default 1 each. Leave the OpenMP stack blank to inherit it, or give a value such as `64M` |
| Memory | NWChem `memory` (`document.nwchem.memory_mb`). Set here and shown in the summaries of “2 Calculation” and “4 Input preview”. The estimated total (multiplied by the process count) is shown. Not an RSS limit |
| Time limit | In seconds; 0 means no limit. When reached, the run is stopped |
| Job folder | Default `~/MolForma/jobs`. Each run gets its own folder named from the calculation name, date and time (local time) and a random ID. Times in `run.json` are recorded in UTC |
| Reference directory | Base for finding relative input movecs. If blank, the project's directory, or the app's working directory for an unsaved project |
| Environment variables | Only enabled rows override the parent environment. Disabled rows are saved but not applied. Names, values and duplicates are checked |

Some MPI-PR builds do not work with 1 MPI process. Specify an NWChem executable and an MPI launcher from the same MPI environment. The UI shows MPI processes and OpenMP/BLAS threads separately, and warns about parallelism beyond the number of logical CPUs and about nested parallelism. Process limits and CPU binding also depend on the MPI configuration. References: [NWChem FAQ](https://nwchemgit.github.io/FAQ.html), [Memory](https://nwchemgit.github.io/Memory.html), [Open MPI mpirun](https://docs.open-mpi.org/en/main/man-openmpi/man1/mpirun.1.html).

### Environment and paths

- `OMP_NUM_THREADS`, `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS`, `GOTO_NUM_THREADS`, `MKL_NUM_THREADS`, `BLIS_NUM_THREADS` and `VECLIB_MAXIMUM_THREADS` are set from dedicated fields. `OMP_DYNAMIC` and `MKL_DYNAMIC` are set to FALSE. The environment variable table rejects duplicate entries for these and for `OMP_STACKSIZE`.
- When `NWCHEM_BASIS_LIBRARY` is given in the table, it is converted to an absolute path relative to the project location and a trailing `/` is added. Other values are passed as strings, without shell or environment variable expansion. Usually you can rely on the basis set configuration of the parent environment and NWChem's `.nwchemrc`. [NWChem Getting Started](https://nwchemgit.github.io/Getting-Started.html)
- The actual working directory is a new job folder each time. Only relative input movecs in the advanced settings are resolved to absolute paths from the reference directory; relative scratch and permanent paths are relative to the job folder. Relative output movecs are relative to permanent, or to the job folder itself when they start with `./`. Output subdirectories inside the job folder are created. Relative output paths that leave the job folder with `../` are rejected. Use absolute paths for locations outside the job folder.
- A run is refused if an output movecs, CUBE or MOLDEN file already exists, or if an external scratch/permanent directory still holds files with the same calculation name. Prepare external directories beforehand. An output movecs file with the same name as the job's input, log or run record is also refused.
- The process is started by `QProcess` with a program name and an argument array; no shell is started. The command field shows POSIX shell notation for review only. Before saving, the date/time and ID are placeholders.

### Saving, running and stopping

“Save as job” (saving the complete input) works even without NWChem installed. It does not copy the executable or referenced movecs files. To run in another environment, prepare the paths, MPI and basis library for that environment.

“Run calculation” saves the same complete input before launching and creates these files:

| File | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| `input.nw` | The complete NWChem input that is run, with relative input movecs paths resolved |
| `project.kgui.json` | A copy of the project with the molecule, calculation settings, advanced settings and run settings at launch |
| `run.json` | Executable, arguments, overriding environment variables, working directory, SHA-256 of the input, times, state, exit code. The full parent environment is not recorded |
| `output.out` / `stderr.err` | Full stdout/stderr, saved directly from the process |

Calculations run asynchronously. Running and saving the complete input are disabled while a candidate is shown or a cleanup is running. After launch the run uses the confirmed copy; editing the molecule or settings, or loading another project, does not affect a running calculation. When the calculation ends, the molecule, coordinates and existing result references are not changed either. The screen shows the last 128 KiB of stdout and stderr; “Open folder” gives access to the full text.

Stopping and the time limit first request termination and kill the process if it does not respond within 2 seconds. On Unix the run gets its own process group, so its child processes are stopped too. On non-Unix systems, stopping sends a termination request to the launched process and then kills it. Closing the window during a run asks you to confirm stopping. Runs do not continue in the background after the app exits.

Launch failure, nonzero exit, stop and time limit are shown and recorded separately. “Exit code 0” means that the process ended normally. When a job that ended normally is loaded into the result viewer, the optimization convergence, calculation energy, number of imaginary frequencies and number of excited states read from the log are shown in one line in “Run status” of the run panel and in the status bar. See the log for the details of each task.

The settings are saved in `document.nwchem_execution` and support the shared Undo/Redo, Ctrl+S and reloading. In “Edit → Preferences” (with the same cards and environment variable table as this tab) you can save to the app settings the defaults for the executable, launch mode, MPI launcher, MPI processes, threads, OpenMP stack, job folder, time limit and additional environment variables, as well as whether to load a water molecule at startup (off by default, so the app starts with a new, empty state), the 3D rendering method and the display language (both take effect after a restart). The defaults are used for new projects and when opening structure files other than projects (XYZ, SDF, PDB, etc.); settings saved in a project are not changed. “Load current run settings” imports the values of the project being shown. Ctrl+S also commits an entry being typed in the environment variable table. The legacy `execution` field, unknown fields, and run and result references are preserved; legacy run paths and options are never applied to NWChem automatically. Applying a calculation preset does not change the run settings.

Currently each window runs one calculation at a time. Queues of multiple jobs with completion notifications, SSH, Slurm/PBS, arbitrary extra MPI arguments, monitoring runs after a restart, and restarts from the NWChem database are not supported. An optimized structure can be brought in with “Apply as candidate” in the “Result viewer”. The MPI-PR build configuration and the contents of the basis library are not detected automatically.

You can start a calculation from any tab with “Run calculation” in the header or “Calculation → Run calculation” (F5) (if it cannot start, “3 Run settings” opens and shows the reason in the check card). The “Calculation” menu also has stop, save as job, open result file and history. When a calculation ends (finished, failed, stopped or time limit), the result is shown in the run status at the right of the status bar (the summary of a log loaded into the result viewer also appears on the left); if MolForma is in the background, the taskbar alerts you, and a desktop notification is shown where supported. With “Load into result viewer when done” enabled (on by default, saved in the app settings), a job folder that ended normally is loaded into the result viewer. The tab does not switch. The log field of the run panel appends only what has been added to the file, so a text selection is not lost (it shows up to the last 128 Ki characters). New lines are shown while “Follow” is on (the default) or while the field is scrolled to the end. The log switch (stdout, stderr), file name, follow and copy are the same as in the file area of “History” (the switch sits below the heading so that the names stay readable in a narrow panel). The state and elapsed time of the running or last calculation are always shown in the status bar at the bottom of the window; clicking it opens the run panel. While running, the header also shows the elapsed time and “Stop”. “Stop” always asks for confirmation, wherever you press it.

The input blocks are on the left, the input in the center, and the input summary, checks and path handling on the right. You can also come here with “Check in “4 Input preview”” in “2 Calculation” and with “Show in input” in the advanced settings.

## “Result viewer”

Load a single result file with “Open…” in the top bar, or list the matching files of a folder with “Open job folder…” in its ▾ menu. From the left, the bar shows the job folder name (the full path in the tooltip), the file list, the format (NWChem output, MOLDEN, CUBE, XYZ) and “Ended normally” if the NWChem log ended normally (without a record of normal termination, or on an error, only the warning above the detail tabs says so), and on the right “Open…”, “Reload”, “Cancel”, “Compare”, “Apply as candidate” and “Export” (only “Open…” while nothing is open). When the window is too narrow for one line, “Compare”, “Apply as candidate” and “Export” become icons only (with their names in tooltips). Loading, cancellation, errors and the result of “Compare” are shown in the message area below the detail tabs on the right; when there is nothing to show, the area is closed (opening and closing it does not move the top edge of the detail tabs). “View results” in the run panel opens the job folder shown there. `output.out` is selected first if present. Only the top level of the folder is scanned. In addition, the CUBE and MOLDEN output paths recorded in MolForma's `run.json` (at most 2) are included in the list. Outputs in permanent subfolders or at absolute paths can be chosen too, but there is no recursive scan.

The file list is grouped by kind: logs, structures, CUBE, MOLDEN. The per-mode, per-step XYZ files written by NWChem `freq animate` (`…m-001.s-001.xyz` etc.) are combined into a single item “Vibration animation …: mode N (K frames)”; choosing it loads all steps joined as one structure sequence (play it with the play button below the 3D view). Choosing another file in the list starts loading it at once and updates the 3D view and details when done. Choosing again while loading cancels the previous load and shows the file chosen last. On failure, the previous display is kept and the reason is shown in the message area below the detail tabs.

| Format | Shown content |
| --- | --- |
| NWChem `.out` / `.log` | Output coordinate tables (Å / a.u.), SCF, DFT, MP2, CCSD and CCSD(T) total energies in output order, optimization convergence messages, termination and error messages (an NWChem errquit box appears in the status as “NWChem error: <message>”), projected frequencies, displacements and IR intensities, optimization “@” steps and convergence thresholds, thermochemistry (ZPE, internal energy and enthalpy corrections per temperature, entropy, Cv, symmetry number), dipole moment, orbital energies of the last orbital analysis, TDDFT excited states (energy, oscillator strength, <S²>), the points of coordinate scans (MolForma's `MolForma scan k/n` markers) and IRC (`&` lines, forward and backward), BSSE correction, COSMO solvation energy and SMD solvation free energy, Mulliken charges from PROPERTY, ESP and RESP charges, NMR shielding constants (isotropic, anisotropy), polarizability tensor |
| `.xyz` | Single and multiple structures. Frame selection and playback of structure sequences. Also concatenated NWChem `freq animate` XYZ |
| `.cube` / `.cub` | Atom coordinates and grid in Bohr, non-orthogonal grids, positive and negative isosurfaces. Orbital CUBE files with a negative atom count and data IDs, selection among multiple data sets with `NVAL` |
| `.molden` / `.mld` / `.molde` | `[Atoms]` (Angs / AU), `[GEOMETRIES] XYZ`, `[GEOCONV] energy`, symmetry, energy, occupation and Alpha/Beta from `[MO]`, `[FREQ]`, `[FR-COORD]`, `[FR-NORM-COORD]`, `[INT]` |

The display is independent of the molecule being edited. Loading files, rotating, zooming, switching frames and playing vibrations do not change the project, fixed atoms, candidate or undo history. All result coordinates are converted to double-precision Å, and bonds are inferred from interatomic distances. The viewer's results, isosurfaces and playback state are not saved in the project. Existing result reference fields are preserved as before.

The 3D view is laid out like the one in “1 Molecule”. “+X”, “+Y”, “+Z”, “Fit to view”, “Rotate” and “View” (labels) at the top right control the viewpoint and labels. “Rotate” pops up the sliders “Left–right”, “Up–down” and “In-plane” (−180 to 180 degrees), which follow mouse operations and +X/+Y/+Z. Only the viewing direction of the structure and isosurfaces changes; the coordinates of saved structures are not affected. The top left shows what is displayed (the structure number with its source line and title, the molecular orbital's name, energy and isovalue, the vibrational mode, the CUBE data and grid).

The control bar below the 3D view changes with what is shown. For structure sequences it has “First structure”, “Play structures”, “Last structure”, the structure number and a timeline (select by clicking, dragging or arrow keys); for the points of coordinate scans and IRC, the timeline draws each point's energy as a line with ΔE relative to the minimum (kcal/mol). For MOLDEN molecular orbitals it has orbital navigation buttons and “HOMO”/“LUMO”; for vibrations, mode playback, amplitude, displacement arrows, export and a “×” that returns to the structure sequence. The control bar keeps its height when its page changes (at narrow widths it wraps by group into 2 rows), so the 3D view does not change size.

Loading, done, cancelled and error messages appear in a fixed-height area at the bottom of the result viewer. Even when the number of message lines changes as you switch orbitals, the 3D view and the arrow buttons do not move up or down. Long paths and full error texts can be scrolled, read and copied within the area.

Choose a vibration in the mode table or by its stick in the IR spectrum, and play it with the play button below the 3D view. Negative frequencies are imaginary. The amplitude is a display value to which the largest atomic displacement is normalized, and the playback speed is not proportional to the actual frequency. Modes without displacements are shown as numbers only. “Export shown structure as XYZ…” under “Export” in the bar writes the selected frame to XYZ, or the reference structure of the vibration while one is shown. “Export 3D view as PNG…” writes the current structure view to PNG.

CUBE positive isosurfaces are shown in red and negative ones in blue. You can change the isovalue, data number, positive/negative display and opacity. Values are in the units recorded in the file; electron density, orbitals, electrostatic potential and so on are not identified automatically. Coordinates and grid use Bohr, as in the Gaussian CUBE files that NWChem writes. The extension that uses a negative grid count to mean Å, and dummy centers with atomic number 0, are rejected. Each axis needs at least 2 points. Isosurfaces are generated with marching cubes. On each face of a cube, the points where the isosurface crosses the edges are joined; a face that can be joined in two ways is resolved by the value at the bilinear saddle point (asymptotic decider), so it matches the face of the neighboring cube and the surface has no gaps. Each edge point is computed once and shared with the neighboring triangles; with VTK the surface is kept as a mesh of shared vertices and drawn with OpenGL (about one third as many triangles as the earlier marching tetrahedra). The QPainter path draws in depth order, so intersections and overlaps of transparent surfaces are approximate. See “3D rendering” for details.

Loading and isosurface generation run in a separate process of the same C++ executable and stop on cancel, on switching to another file and when the window closes. On failure or cancellation the previous result is kept. A file whose size, modification time or link target changed during loading is not accepted. Update the log of a running calculation with “Reload” (the log is followed automatically in the run panel and in “History”). With “Load into result viewer when done” enabled in “3 Run settings”, the job folder is loaded when the calculation ends. “Normal termination marker found” does not guarantee that all tasks succeeded or converged. Optimization convergence, the calculation energy, the number of imaginary frequencies and so on are shown in “Summary” (MCP's `get_app_state` returns them in one line in its `message`, along with the termination state). If the NWChem log has no record of normal termination or an error is detected, a warning appears above the detail tabs, and “Show end of log” jumps to the end of “Source file”. The detail tabs (Energy, Orbitals, Vibrations, Optimization, Excited states, Path / scan, Properties, CUBE / isosurface, Source file) appear only when the loaded file has data for them, and their names include a count (records, orbitals, vibrations excluding translations and rotations, optimization steps, excited states, path points). When nothing is open, “Summary” describes the files that can be opened, and the 3D view controls (+X/+Y/+Z, Fit to view, Rotate, View) and the structure switching below the 3D view are hidden. View, rotation and measurement controls are disabled until a structure is open. Energies and structures are each listed in output order; a one-to-one mapping to optimization steps is not assumed. The calculation energy (“Energy” in “Summary”) is found by splitting the log by task, and is the value of the last task. For a frequency task it is the value of the reference structure (because a numerical Hessian then prints the energies of displaced structures), and for a BSSE correction it is the corrected value.

Loading limits are 128 MiB per file, 2000 atoms per structure, 2000 structures and 200,000 atoms in total, 2 million CUBE grid points and 4 million values over all data sets, and 500,000 isosurface triangles. Exceeding a limit gives an explicit error. If an isosurface is too large, change the isovalue in “CUBE / isosurface” and reopen the file, or use a smaller grid. “Source file” shows the last 128 KiB.

“Summary” shows cards with the formula of the last structure, the calculation energy, geometry optimization (convergence, step count, number of criteria met by the last step), imaginary frequencies (below −10 cm⁻¹), HOMO–LUMO gap (eV, per spin), dipole moment (Debye), excited states, coordinate scan and IRC, isosurface and structure sequence. Pressing a card opens the corresponding detail tab. Below them follow the thermochemical corrections per temperature and the Gibbs correction G = H − TS (computed by MolForma; add it to the electronic energy), other results (BSSE, solvation, polarizability, etc.), the log state and warnings, and the result files in the same folder (switch with “Open”). The “Optimization” tab shows the last step's Gmax, Grms, Xrms and Xmax next to the convergence thresholds, the energy change relative to the last step (kcal/mol) and log plots of Gmax and Grms (dashed lines are the convergence thresholds), and a step table (values that meet the thresholds are colored; displacements are not judged at the first step). The “Excited states” tab shows a list of the states and an absorption spectrum for display, made by broadening the oscillator strengths with Gaussians (FWHM in eV), on a wavelength or energy axis. Orbitals from a log have only energies and occupations; showing their shapes requires MOLDEN.

“Apply as candidate” applies the coordinates of the shown structure (the reference structure while a vibration plays) to the molecule in “1 Molecule” as a candidate and switches to “1 Molecule”. It accepts only results whose atom count and order, elements and ghosts match, and refuses results in which a fixed atom has moved by more than 0.001 Å (to guard against results in a different coordinate frame). Atom IDs, bonds, residues and calculation settings are not changed, and the molecule is not changed until “Confirm changes”. The RMSD and largest displacement are shown in the status bar.

The “Path / scan” tab shows the points of a coordinate scan (value, energy, ΔE relative to the minimum / kcal mol⁻¹) or of an IRC (reaction coordinate s / amu½·Bohr with the transition state at 0, positive forward and negative backward, ΔE relative to the transition state) as a table and a plot; selecting a row shows that point's structure. The “Properties” tab shows a table of per-atom Mulliken, ESP and RESP charges and NMR shielding constants and anisotropies, plus the polarizability (a.u. and Å³), BSSE correction and solvation energy. NMR values are absolute shielding constants; chemical shifts need the difference from a reference compound computed under the same conditions.

The “Energy” tab shows total energies (Eh, 10 decimals) and ΔE relative to the selected row in kcal/mol, kJ/mol, eV, Hartree and cm⁻¹ (the default reference is the last row). Differences between rows of different methods are meaningless. The “Vibrations” tab labels the 6 modes with the smallest absolute frequencies (5 for linear molecules) as “Translation/rotation” and the other negative frequencies as “Imaginary” (when all 3N modes are present). Translations and rotations are left out of the list by default (IR intensity “—”) and are shown with “Show translations and rotations”. The number and values of imaginary frequencies in the summary also exclude them. IR intensities in the mode table are also shown as bars with lengths relative to the strongest mode. The IR spectrum is a display curve that broadens the IR intensities, excluding translations, rotations and imaginary modes, with Lorentzians of the given FWHM, drawn with higher wavenumbers on the left. Clicking a stick selects that mode, and the stick of the selected mode is highlighted. “Arrows” below the 3D view overlays the displacements of the selected mode on the reference structure (the longest arrow is three times the amplitude); under “Export”, “Export vibration as image sequence…” writes one period as 24 PNG images and “Export vibration as multi-frame XYZ…” writes it as a 24-frame XYZ.

Clicking atoms in the result 3D view (2–4 atoms) shows the distance, angle or dihedral in the area at the bottom left of the 3D view; “Pin” keeps it on screen (“Clear” removes the selection and the measurements shown). Measurements are kept by atom number, so they follow switching structures and playing vibrations. They are cleared when another file is opened. “Compare” superimposes the shown structure on the molecule in “1 Molecule” and shows the RMSD and the largest deviation (same atoms in the same order).

Parsing logs of arbitrary formats is not supported. Orbital shapes can be checked with MOLDEN files or molecular orbital CUBE files written by NWChem. The vibration sections of a MOLDEN file are shown when you open a file that contains that data. It is not assumed that `MOLDENFILE` alone also writes vibration sections.

### Showing molecular orbitals from MOLDEN

The “Orbitals” tab for an NWChem log shows only orbital energies, numbered with NWChem's orbital numbers (the same numbers as in MOLDEN even if the log lists only some orbitals). If the same job folder has a MOLDEN file, “Show shape” or double-clicking an orbital row opens that MOLDEN file and shows the same orbital (the orbital with the same spin and NWChem number; the Alpha HOMO if none is selected). MOLDEN files are written with “Write MOLDEN file” under “Orbitals and output files” in “2 Calculation”. Opening a MOLDEN file with `[Atoms]`, `[GTO]` and `[MO]` shows the Alpha HOMO automatically and moves to the “Orbitals” tab. Without occupied Alpha orbitals Beta is chosen, and without occupied orbitals the first orbital. The HOMO is the highest-energy orbital with positive occupation and the LUMO the lowest-energy unoccupied orbital, determined per spin. Degenerate and arbitrary orbitals can also be chosen from the list.

- The level diagram on the left of the “Orbitals” tab draws, per spin, up to 6 levels below the HOMO and above the LUMO (within the range from 25 eV below the HOMO to 15 eV above the LUMO). Occupied orbitals are lines with electron arrows, unoccupied ones are boxes, and levels at nearly the same energy (degenerate orbitals) are placed side by side. With one spin, the names and energies of the HOMO and LUMO and the HOMO–LUMO gap are added. Orbitals outside the range are only counted (so that core orbitals do not squeeze the valence levels). Clicking a level selects the orbital as in the list, and hovering over it shows its number, energy and occupation. Orbitals from a log get the same diagram. The list omits the spin column when there is only one spin, and the symmetry column when all symmetry labels are the same.
- “HOMO” and “LUMO” (in the control bar below the 3D view) show that orbital of the selected spin. Clicking a list row or selecting with the up/down keys switches the display at once. If you choose again while loading, the orbital chosen last is shown. Orbital numbers appear only in the “No.” column and count from 1 for Alpha and Beta separately.
- The control bar below the 3D view has the arrow buttons “First orbital”, “Back 10 orbitals”, “Previous orbital”, “Next orbital”, “Forward 10 orbitals” and “Last orbital”. They move in list order from the shown orbital, also across the Alpha/Beta boundary. If fewer than 10 orbitals remain, they stop at the first or last orbital. The spin and number of the shown orbital appear between “Previous orbital” and “Next orbital”, and its position relative to the HOMO or LUMO (HOMO−1, etc.) and its energy at the top left of the 3D view.
- Positive orbital values are drawn in red and negative ones in blue. The overall sign of the phase is arbitrary. The default isovalue is ±0.03 bohr⁻³ᐟ², and you can change the value and redisplay. Positive/negative display and opacity are adjusted in “CUBE / isosurface”.
- The grid spans the minimum and maximum atom coordinates on each axis plus 3 Å, divides the longest edge into 40, and uses the same spacing on the other axes (the same grid as the default for CUBE files written by MolForma). It can be adjusted between 16 and 100 divisions. This is a viewing setting, independent of the CUBE grid settings for input generation.
- The normalization of the basis coefficients is identified as standard MOLDEN or the NWChem convention from the analytic self-overlap integrals of the contracted functions, and the convention used is shown. The standard convention multiplies by the primitive normalization factors, the NWChem convention uses the factors included in the coefficients, and the contracted functions are normalized. If it cannot be identified, the shape is not shown and you choose the convention and redisplay.
- The isovalue and the number of grid divisions apply to the selected orbital when committed with Enter or by leaving the field. The normalization applies when its selection changes.
- s, p and sp, Cartesian 6d/10f/15g and real spherical harmonic 5d/7f/9g functions are supported. The basis function order of the MOLDEN convention is used. Shapes cannot be shown for STO, h and higher functions or complex orbitals, or when basis functions or coefficients are missing or inconsistent. Structure and orbital information is shown as far as possible, with the reason.

Orbital evaluation and isosurface generation run in a separate native C++ process; neither NWChem, Python nor CUBE generation is needed. Cancelling loads and rejecting stale results work as for normal result loading. The number of the shown orbital is stated explicitly, and if recomputation fails, the previous display is kept. When you switch to another structure frame or vibration of the MOLDEN file, the surface is not overlaid at positions different from the orbital's reference structure. The molecule being edited, Undo and the source file are not changed.

Orbital evaluation reuses Gaussian exponentials and coordinate powers along each axis, which makes it faster without lowering the grid density. The grid is split by x slices among several threads (each grid point's value is the same regardless of the split). Shown isosurfaces are kept in memory up to about 64 MiB, and returning to the same orbital, isovalue, grid divisions and normalization shows them again without recomputation. Beyond the limit, the oldest cache entries are released. The cache is discarded when the file's path, link target, size, modification time or metadata change time changes, when the file is reopened, and on “Reload”. Orbitals not yet shown or released from the cache take time to compute.

Limits are 4000 basis functions, 10,000 primitives, 100 primitives per shell and 4 million MO coefficients. If grid points × primitives exceeds 1 billion, you are advised to reduce the divisions. Recomputing the total electron density or electrostatic potential is not supported. The total energy is shown only if MOLDEN's `[GEOCONV]` has a value; check the total energy of a regular NWChem calculation in `output.out`.

References for the conventions: [MOLDEN format](https://www.theochem.ru.nl/molden/molden_format.html), [MOLDEN normalization in NWChem](https://nwchemgit.github.io/Properties.html#moldenfile).

### Example: generating CUBE and MOLDEN files with NWChem

In the GUI, enabling CUBE and MOLDEN output under “Orbitals and output files” in “2 Calculation” generates the directives automatically. If you edit the input by hand outside MolForma, add directives like the following after the SCF/DFT orbitals are computed. The example is for HF with the calculation name `water`, the orbital file `water.movecs` and Alpha orbital 5. Adjust the calculation, orbital number, file names and spatial range to your case. For DFT, change `task scf property` to `task dft property`.

```text
property
  moldenfile
  molden_norm nwchem
end
task scf property

dplot
  vectors water.movecs
  limitxyz units angstrom
    -3 3 24
    -3 3 24
    -3 3 24
  spin alpha
  orbitals view; 1; 5
  gaussian
  output orbital5.cube
end
task dplot
```

Omitting `orbitals view` and specifying `spin total` produces a CUBE of the total electron density. The 24 in `limitxyz` is the number of divisions along each direction, giving 25 grid points. Choose the normalization convention to suit the program that will read the MOLDEN file. References: [NWChem DPLOT](https://nwchemgit.github.io/DPLOT.html), [NWChem Properties / MOLDENFILE](https://nwchemgit.github.io/Properties.html#moldenfile), [MOLDEN format](https://www.theochem.ru.nl/molden/molden_format.html), [VMD CUBE reader](https://www.ks.uiuc.edu/Research/vmd/plugins/molfile/cubeplugin.html).

## History (NWChem)

“History” reads the jobs you saved or ran (“Save as job”, “Run calculation”) from the `run.json` files directly under the job folder. After a restart, choosing the same folder restores the list. By default it uses the folder of “3 Run settings”. The job folder button at the top shows the path and whether it is “(same as 3 Run settings)” or a “(chosen folder)” (when the toolbar does not fit on one row, the caption “Job folder” and this note move into the tooltip, the search field narrows and “Compare ΔE” shows only its icon); another folder chosen with “Choose folder…” in its menu is remembered in the app settings. “Back to the “3 Run settings” folder” returns to following it. Viewing the history does not change the molecule being edited, candidates, Undo or the unsaved state of the project.

- **List**: Shows the state (a colored label), the calculation name and composition (the molecular formula at calculation time; a job left with the default calculation name “molforma” is listed by its title), method and basis set (the functional name for DFT, HF for Hartree–Fock), task, creation time (local time), duration, the calculation energy (Eh, with the method) and the note. Sorted by creation time, the list is divided by day (“Today”, “Yesterday” or the date, with the count); sorted by another column header, it is not divided. Rows without a value go last whatever the sort order. Search covers the name, method, basis set, task, composition, note, folder and state. The switch at the top filters by state (All, Running, Finished, Stopped / failed, Saved only; each count is the number of jobs matching the search). “Running” includes unconfirmed jobs, and “Stopped / failed” includes interrupted, no-process and unreadable records. Sorting and refreshing keep the selection by folder, and actions on rows hidden by the filter are disabled.
- **Selected job (right)**: Shows the calculation name and state; method, basis set and task; composition, atom count, charge and multiplicity (read from the project saved with the job); the calculation energy and its kind (last task, frequency reference structure, BSSE-corrected, or last value in the log tail); start, end and duration; the exit code and whether normal termination was logged; the integrity check; run errors; warnings; the folder (its path can be copied); the note; and the actions. The note is saved to `memo.txt` in the job's folder shortly after you stop typing or when you move to another field (it is not covered by the integrity hashes; up to 1000 characters). The CSV also contains the composition and note.
- **Running jobs**: When you select a job running in this window, it shows, instead of the energy, the same progress as the run panel (done, running and waiting for each task; the latest geometry optimization step with the Gmax, Grms, Xmax and Xrms values and thresholds) with the elapsed time. You can use “Stop this calculation” (with confirmation), “Open log so far” (result viewer) and “Show run status and log” (run panel). While a job runs, the reuse, note and trash actions are hidden.
- **Files (below the list)**: “Input”, “stdout”, “stderr” and “Record” switch between the fixed copies made at calculation time, and you can copy the shown file. The input is `input.nw` itself, not regenerated from the current edits. Logs show their last 128 KiB; with “Follow” on, they refresh every second while shown. Full logs are available through “Open folder”. The arrow left of the heading expands and collapses the section (in the same way as the other cards; collapsed at first so the list is wider; the state is remembered in the app settings). In windows narrower than 1250 px, the right-hand column is hidden until you select a job so the list is wider, stays narrow after you select one, and the list shows “Task” under “Method / basis”.
- “Open in result viewer” or double-clicking a row opens the results of the selected job. “Show in history” in the run panel selects the job shown there.
- “Reuse molecule and settings” validates the saved `project.kgui.json` and then opens it as a new unsaved project. If there are unsaved edits, the usual save/discard/cancel dialog appears. Reuse is not possible while a candidate or cleanup is pending. The input and project of the original job are never overwritten; to run again, “Run calculation” starts a new job. The coordinates loaded are the molecule before the calculation. To take the optimized structure, follow with “Open in result viewer” → “Apply as candidate”.
- “Export list as CSV…” in the “…” menu at the top exports all shown rows with the current filter and sort order. Times are written in UTC ISO format, numbers with `.` as the decimal point, in UTF-8. Strings that could be read as formulas get a leading `'`.
- “Stop this calculation” stops, after confirmation, only runs managed by this window. “Move to trash” moves, after confirmation, the whole folder of the selected job to the OS trash. Running and unconfirmed jobs and broken run records are excluded (“Interrupted, no process” jobs can be moved).
- **Repairing records**: When there are “Interrupted, no process” jobs, a bar above the list shows their count and calculation names; “Repair record” there changes all those records to “Interrupted (record repaired)” and computes the calculation energy from the log. You can also repair only the selected job with “Repair record” on the right. A folder without trash support gives an error; MolForma does not switch to permanent deletion.
- **Comparing ΔE**: “Compare ΔE”, or “Compare ΔE with this job” on the right, adds a column with the difference (ΔE) from the reference job and sorts by ΔE. The unit is kcal/mol, kJ/mol, eV or Eh, shown as a number and a bar; the reference row reads “Reference”. ΔE is shown only for jobs with the same composition, method (including the DFT functional), basis set and kind of energy (with or without BSSE correction, etc.) as the reference; other jobs show the reason (such as “Basis set differs”). Running and unconfirmed jobs show “Shown when finished”, “Interrupted, no process” jobs “Shown after repair”, and jobs without an energy “No energy recorded”. The right side shows the difference for the selected job in all four units, with both energies and the kind of value compared. Thermal corrections (zero-point energy, etc.) are not included. While comparing, the “Files” section is collapsed; “Use as reference” changes the reference and “Stop comparing” returns to the normal list.
- **Folders without jobs**: For a folder with no jobs (or one that is missing or unreadable), guidance with “Choose folder…” and “Back to 3 Run settings folder” replaces the list, and the number of jobs (up to 2000) in the folder of “3 Run settings” is shown.

The list shows the state as a short label (Saved only, Starting, Running, Stopping, Finished, Failed, Stopped, Timed out, Unconfirmed, No process, Interrupted, Unreadable); the detailed state (“Input saved”, “Finished (code 0)”, “Run failed”, “Stopped”, “Not confirmed running (recorded: running)”, “Interrupted, no process”, “Interrupted (record repaired)”, “Record unreadable”, etc.) appears in the tooltip of the row and of the label on the right. `run.json` records the host name, boot ID (Linux), process ID and process start time of the run. A calculation left recorded as `starting` / `running` / `stopping` by another window or an earlier session is marked “Interrupted, no process” only when it was recorded on the same PC and its process can be shown to have ended (the PID no longer exists, has been reused by another process, or the PC has restarted); otherwise (another PC, an old record without process information, or still running) it is “Unconfirmed”. Exit code 0 does not mean convergence or success of all tasks. The energy in the list is the value found at the end of the calculation by dividing the whole log by task and stored in `summary` of `run.json` (the SCF, DFT, MP2, CCSD or CCSD(T) total energy of the last task; for a frequency task the value of the reference structure, for BSSE the corrected value). Running jobs, and older jobs without this record, show the last total energy within the last 128 KiB of the log. It is not necessarily a converged value. If there is no such value, `—` is shown.

Newly saved `run.json` files also contain a summary of the calculation settings, the base directory of the original project and the SHA-256 of the project. Reuse is refused when the input or project does not match. Older version 1 records are read too; when the settings summary is missing, it is taken from the fixed copies. When an old record has no project hash, only the input is checked, and this is shown. The hashes are there to detect external edits and corruption; they are not signatures of authenticity.

Reuse keeps the original reference directory and resolves a relative executable path from the original base. The new job is saved in the parent folder of the selected job. If an old record cannot restore the original location of a relative executable path, reuse is refused. External movecs, basis set, scratch and permanent directories are neither copied nor guessed for the new location. Check in the run settings that they exist and are writable before running again. Not supported: converting a calculation whose input was edited by hand back to the original settings, restarting from the NWChem database, SSH, job queues, stopping processes of other windows, and history graphs.

The list is built by scanning the jobs directly under the folder one at a time in the event loop, and the scan can be stopped or switched to another folder. The limit is 2000 jobs or 20000 folders; when it is reached, a partial list and a limit message are shown. Subfolders are not scanned recursively, and jobs reached through symbolic links are excluded. `run.json` is parsed up to 256 KiB, the input and project up to 8 MiB each. Folders with broken records remain as error rows, and the rest of the history is still read. Folders that hold only an arbitrary NWChem output are not registered in the history; open them directly in the “Result viewer”.

## Whole application

- **Common building blocks**: Every tab is built from the same parts: a top row (toolbar), white cards, switches (segments for up to 5 choices; underlined tabs for more, as in the details of the result viewer; tabs are never placed inside tabs), buttons (blue for the main action of the page, red outline for delete and trash, blue text with an arrow for moving to another tab), check-result cards (a heading such as “Input can be generated” or “Ready to run” and items), calculation summary cards, log and file views (switch, file name, follow, copy), tables (no grid lines; numbers monospaced and right-aligned) and the controls at the top right of the 3D view.
- **One place to edit each value**: Other tabs show the value and lead there with a link such as “Change in “2 Calculation””. Total charge, multiplicity, whether external point charges go into the input, the coordinate output unit, and MOLDEN and CUBE output are set in “2 Calculation”; memory in “3 Run settings”; and the table of formal charges, unpaired electrons and atom names with “Attributes” in the coordinate table of “1 Molecule”.
- **Status bar**: A single line at the bottom of the window. Normally it shows a summary of the molecule being edited (formula · atoms · bonds · charge · multiplicity · point charges); during a temporary message it shows only that sentence (the summary is hidden, and nothing overlaps when the display at the right end changes). In the result viewer and history, the summary starts with “Molecule being edited:” so that it is not mistaken for the shown result. The right end shows the state of the running or last calculation (click it for the run panel) and similar information.
- **Notation**: Energies are written in Eh (= hartree) on screen (the column names of the history CSV stay as before for compatibility). Labels use sentence case. Words in a list are joined with “, ” (or “and”), and items of a summary are separated by “ · ” with a space on each side. An action that creates one file is named “Export …”, one that reads from a file “Import …” or “Load …”, one that creates a job folder “Save as job”, one that runs the checks again “Recheck”, the view that fits the whole molecule “Fit to view”, and fixing atoms “Fix selected atoms” and “Unfix”. Tabs are named by number and tab name in text and in links alike (in text in quotes, as “2 Calculation”; a link to another tab reads Check in “4 Input preview”). Only the workflow steps in the header show the number as a round badge. The minus sign of values meant to be read, such as negative energies, is “−” (U+2212); the table cells of the result viewer use “-” so that copied values stay numbers.
- **Recent files**: “File → Recent files” lists up to 10 files you opened or saved, newest first (stored in the app settings). Missing files cannot be chosen. “Clear list” clears it.
- **Display language (Japanese, English)**: The windows, messages, guide and manual can be shown in Japanese or English. By default MolForma follows the system language (Japanese in a Japanese environment, English otherwise); “Language (after restart)” in “Edit → Preferences…” fixes it to 日本語 or English (it takes effect after a restart). The environment variable `MOLFORMA_LANGUAGE` (`ja` / `en`), when set, takes precedence. Messages of the cleanup and result-loading workers and of MCP responses use the same language. The contents of projects, jobs and NWChem input are the same whatever the display language. The English translations are in `assets/translations/en.json`, and a test (`translations`) checks that every Japanese string in the source is covered by the translation and has a translation.
- **Language of Qt dialogs**: Qt's standard dialogs and buttons, such as message boxes and file dialogs, follow the display language as well. In Japanese, Qt's translations (`qt6-translations` on Arch Linux, `qt6-translations-l10n` on Debian/Ubuntu, etc.) are used when installed; without them, the standard buttons such as 保存 (Save), 変更を破棄 (Discard), キャンセル (Cancel) and 閉じる (Close) are still shown in Japanese.
- **New**: “File → New” creates an empty unsaved project and does not ask about saving until you edit it (the run settings are the defaults from “Preferences”). It also resets the view to the front. The default file name when saving is the molecular formula (e.g. `C2H6O.kgui.json`).
- **Load errors**: For a file that cannot be opened, the file name and the reason are shown. For XYZ, the number and text of the offending line are shown.
- **Copying and pasting XYZ**: “File → Copy whole molecule as XYZ” copies the whole molecule, and “Edit → Copy selection as XYZ” (Ctrl+C) the selected atoms (the whole molecule if nothing is selected). “File → Replace with pasted XYZ…” makes a candidate that replaces the current structure, and “Edit → Paste XYZ as fragment” (Ctrl+V) a candidate that adds it beside the current molecule.
- **Drag and drop**: Dropping a structure file, a project or NWChem/Gaussian input on the window opens it (asking about unsaved changes). Dropping XYZ, MOL, SDF, PDB or ENT on the coordinate table makes a candidate that replaces the structure instead of opening it. `.out`, `.log`, CUBE, MOLDEN and folders open in the result viewer. Dropping a part from the palette of “1 Molecule” places it on the molecule as before.
- **Autosave and recovery**: In interactive sessions, while there are unsaved changes, a copy of the project is saved every minute to the data folder (`~/.local/share/MolForma/autosave/` on Linux). Saving or exiting normally deletes the copy. If a copy from a session that ended abnormally remains, the next start shows its save time, original file and molecule and asks whether to “Recover”, “Discard” or “Decide later”. A recovered molecule opens as an unsaved project, so save it under a name. Copies in use by other MolForma windows are left untouched. `--mcp-gui` sessions autosave but do not ask about recovery (`--mcp` and `--screenshot` are not covered).
- **Guide and manual**: “Help → Guide and manual” (F1) shows this file, the [recipes by topic](recipes.md) (16 recipes with screenshots) and the MCP description, built into the executable (no installed documentation files are needed). The English interface shows this English manual. Links between the pages can be followed, and “Back” returns to the previous page. Pressing Enter in the search field moves to the next match.
- **Desktop integration**: `cmake --install` installs the launcher (`share/applications/molforma.desktop`), the icon (`molforma` in `share/icons/hicolor`) and the MIME types for projects (`*.kgui.json`) and NWChem input (`*.nw`) (`share/mime/packages/molforma.xml`). Updating the system databases may need `update-desktop-database` / `update-mime-database`. The window icon uses the same design. `LICENSE` (GNU GPL version 2; MolForma is version 2 or later), `NOTICE` and `README.md` are installed in `share/molforma`.

## MCP server

`--mcp` (no window) and `--mcp-gui` (sharing the document of the launched GUI) provide a native stdio server. It supports the per-request version selection and `server/discover` of MCP **2026-07-28**, the latest stable version as of 2026-10-24, and the initialization of **2025-11-25**.

23 common tools expose getting the document and parts, editing atoms, bonds and coordinates, setting dimensions (distance, angle, dihedral), changing atom attributes (element, formal charge, unpaired electrons, isotope, mass, fixed, ghost, atom name), measuring, loading and saving files, confirming and discarding candidates and Undo/Redo, checking and changing NWChem basic, advanced, output and run settings, and generating input. Edits specify the document ID and the content version, and conflicts with changes on the GUI side are refused. Fixed atoms and unknown project fields are protected, and changes are validated before they are added as a candidate or to the history.

`--mcp` adds 4 calculation and result tools, 27 tools in total. It can run and stop NWChem and save complete inputs, and load, cancel loading of and read result files and calculation folders. Calculations stop when the process exits.

`--mcp-gui` has 40 tools in total. It exposes SMILES generation, adding fragments, cleanup, conformer search and cancelling them; saving complete NWChem inputs, running and stopping; scanning history, reuse, trash and CSV; loading results, cancelling, and getting structure sequences, orbitals, frequencies and displacements; playing and stopping modes, including imaginary ones, and structure sequences; and getting PNG images. Other existing GUI operations work too: listing widgets, triggering buttons and menus, entering values, selecting tabs, tables and trees, editing cells, and mouse and key input within Qt. Confirmation and file dialogs can be handled over MCP, so no OS-level UI automation is needed.

GUI changes return an operation ID, and messages are accepted while a dialog is open. Editing the document in the background and operations on destroyed widgets are refused. Because the confirmation, candidate and prompt behavior of the GUI carries over, check the completion of an operation and the completion of a calculation or load separately. Result and camera operations do not change the edited document. Only `apply_structure` of `results_action` turns the shown result structure into a candidate of the document, which `confirm` of `history` confirms.

Claude Code can register it with `claude mcp add` or a shared `.mcp.json`, and passes on the desktop environment variables as they are. Version 2.1.310 connects with the new specification and uses the Tasks extension in interactive sessions. See the [Claude Code configuration example](mcp.md#registering-in-claude-code).

For GUI integration with Codex on Linux, set `DISPLAY` / `WAYLAND_DISPLAY` / `XDG_RUNTIME_DIR` / `XAUTHORITY` etc. in `env_vars` to pass on the connection to the desktop. If no display is specified, MolForma writes how to set it to standard error before Qt is initialized and exits. See the [Codex configuration example](mcp.md#registering-in-codex).

Over MCP, the NWChem executable, MPI launcher, extra environment variables and additional calculation input cannot be changed, and calculations started over MCP run only with the values the user set (`--mcp-allow-execution-settings` lifts this). With `--mcp-allow-dir`, the places MCP reads and writes are confined to that directory. See [Safety restrictions](mcp.md#safety-restrictions).

The standard error of the calculation (`molforma://calculation/stderr`) can also be read as a resource. Prompts (4 workflows), Elicitation for the overwrite confirmation of `save_file`, the Tasks extension (tracking calculations, cleanup, loading and history scans to completion, cancelling with `tasks/cancel`, answering confirmation dialogs) and resources for the document, calculation output and results are supported. Every tool has a `title` and an `outputSchema`. Currently only POSIX stdio is supported. HTTP/Windows transports, subscriptions, Sampling, etc. are not supported. Limits: 8 MiB per received message and per structure file loaded, 10,000 atoms per document, 100 headless Undo steps. Result loading uses the existing limits of the viewer, and GUI and result listings are split into pages of about 40 KiB (up to 2 MiB for a single item). Editing tools return a summary of the change, not the whole document. Closing standard input also ends GUI mode, so save the documents you need before disconnecting. After an update, reconnect the MCP client to pick up added tools. See [Connection settings and details](mcp.md).

## Limitations and unsupported features

The main limitations and unsupported features are:

- PDB repair: “Residue → PDB diagnosis and repair…” diagnoses each residue, makes candidates that complete missing atoms and recognizes residues again; you check and confirm them in the main window. Completion covers residues with the backbone N/CA/C and a standard amino-acid template; unknown residues and missing backbones are not repaired automatically. The MODEL/ALTLOC choice, atom names and chain information are kept.
- Fragment assignment (FMO, counterpoise, SAPT0) and its validation. As it cannot be converted to NWChem input, there is no UI to create or edit it ([“1 Molecule” right-hand pages and NWChem](#right-side-pages-of-1-molecule-and-nwchem-support)). Assignments in legacy projects are only kept. Counterpoise correction can be calculated with the BSSE correction of “2 Calculation” (one monomer per bonded molecule). FMO and SAPT0 are not supported.
- Migration of v1 projects.

Assigning and recording R/S and E/Z and recognizing residues from the bond graph use the optional RDKit C++ library. It is enabled in this environment. Where it is not found, the corresponding buttons are disabled.

The 3D view supports C++ VTK/OpenGL and a QPainter fallback renderer. Cleanup and SMILES generation use Open Babel. Force-field cleanup of metals, coordination bonds and ghosts is refused. Main-group elements such as Br, I, Se and Te can be cleaned up if MMFF94 or UFF has parameters for them. Attaching My parts supports one attachment point, and ligands 1 or 2 donor atoms. My parts can also be saved as “Skeleton (no attachment point, placed in empty space)” and are then placed by clicking empty space, like the built-in skeletons.

“File → Add fragment from SMILES…” and “Add fragment from file…” (XYZ, MOL, SDF, Mol2, CML, PDB, NWChem and Gaussian input) keep the current molecule and add another molecule 3 Å away on the +X side as a candidate (for dimers, solvation, arrangements before complex formation, etc.). Atoms, bonds, residues, parts and coordination centers are taken over; the point charges and calculation settings of the fragment are not. If the total charge matched the sum of formal charges, it is updated to the sum after adding. Adjust the position with “Translate” or by setting dimensions between unbonded atoms. For multi-frame XYZ, you choose the frame to add.

The run settings and result references of existing projects are kept too. Constraints and scans of legacy projects are saved as they are in the `section/key/text/ids/span` form and converted to the new numbers when atoms are reordered. Settings invalidated by deletion are saved as pending. Legacy fragment assignments are kept in both `document` and `job.sections`, and point charges are also written to `job.sections.qmmm` only when a legacy QM/MM section exists. None of these are used in NWChem input.

## Worked examples

### Ala–Gly–Ser

1. Open a new molecule and enter `AGS` as the sequence in “Residue”.
2. Set the N-terminus to protonated and the C-terminus to COO−, and press “Build from sequence”.
3. Check that the candidate is **C8H15N3O5, 31 atoms, formal charge 0**, and confirm it.
4. In “2 Calculation”, choose the method, basis set and task (e.g. HF / STO-3G, single-point energy). The total charge is 0, the same as the sum of formal charges.
5. Check the input in “4 Input preview” and start the calculation with “Run calculation” in the header.

### Tetrahedral zinc complex

1. Choose Zn in the coordination-center palette.
2. In “Coordination”, set the geometry to tetrahedral, the distance to 2.3 Å and the oxidation state to 2.
3. Click empty space to place the center.
4. Choose chloride in the ligand palette, and place and confirm it on sites 1–4 in turn.
5. In “Charge and spin” of “2 Calculation”, set the total charge of the calculation to −2 and the multiplicity to 1 (also shown on the “Charge” page of “1 Molecule”).

### Counterpoise (BSSE) correction of the water dimer

1. Place the water skeleton, add a second water on the +X side with “File → Add fragment from file…” or “Add fragment from SMILES…” (`O`), and confirm it.
2. Select a hydrogen of one water and then the oxygen of the other, and set the H···O distance to about 1.95 Å with the dimension in “Edit” (as they are separate, unbonded molecules, the whole water selected last moves). “View → Show hydrogen bonds” shows the dashed line.
3. In “2 Calculation”, choose the method and basis set, and turn on “Counterpoise correction per monomer (BSSE)” in “BSSE correction”. The two monomers are shown.
4. Run it with “Run calculation”, and check the BSSE correction and the corrected energy in “Properties” or the summary of the “Result viewer”. If you choose geometry optimization, it optimizes with BSSE-corrected gradients.
